Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 11 Review: The Price of Himawari's Power Is More Interesting Than Its Size

Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 11, True Power: an in-depth, spoiler-heavy analysis of Himawari versus Jura, Inojin's recovery, Sarada's danger and the limits of Boruto's Flying Thunder God rescue.

Source-based review by USA Anime Club Editorial · Research checked November 15, 2026 · Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Spoiler notice: this article discusses released installment events described by the sources.

THE SOURCED TAKEAWAY

True Power shows why emotional motivation cannot instantly replace experience. Himawari's frightening potential is real, but Jura's control exposes her limits. The chapter also raises a fair concern about how cheaply Inojin's previous injury is resolved.

The most important display of strength is a decision to send friends away

The title True Power almost asks to be interpreted as a competition: how much of Kurama's energy can Himawari release, and how close is that to Jura's strength? Yet the reported action begins with a quieter choice. Newly awakened and furious about Inojin's injury, Himawari asks her friends to put distance between themselves and the fight. Chocho resists because abandoning her friend feels wrong. Shikadai recognizes that remaining in range of Jura may turn devotion into another casualty. Even the antagonist notices his cool judgment. The episode is strongest when it presents this as a conflict between two legitimate kinds of care rather than declaring one character cowardly or another foolish.

Himawari has already been targeted precisely because she contains power she does not understand. The temptation would be to make her awakening a declaration that she no longer needs anyone. Instead, the danger to Team 10 forces her to consider how power changes responsibility. Someone capable of extraordinary damage can also endanger the people who stand closest to her. Shikadai's tactical retreat does not diminish their friendship; it is the only way to allow an inexperienced fighter room to attempt something without multiplying the stakes. That makes the scene a credible extension of Team 10's protection in Chapters 9 and 10, rather than a convenient arrangement designed only to isolate the combatants.

Inojin's recovery is welcome, and dramatically inconvenient

Contemporary criticism describes Himawari drawing on new healing ability to stabilize Inojin and withdrawing the young ninja from immediate danger. The relief is understandable. His injury at the end of the preceding chapter was severe enough to make readers fear for him. Nobody needs a story to kill a teenage character merely to prove the villains are dangerous. But the speed of his recovery creates a storytelling problem that should not be ignored. If an apparent fatal injury can be reversed almost as soon as its emotional effect has served its purpose, the author needs to establish limits before the next shocking wound.

Here the reported dialogue provides some restraint: Himawari is not certain of what she can do, and professional medical attention remains necessary. That caveat prevents her from becoming an all-purpose medical solution, though it may not fully restore the previous chapter's tension. A more lasting consequence could emerge through Inojin's own responses, Team 10's fear, or a cost to Himawari's chakra reserves. What matters is that healing should change the situation without pretending the injury never happened. The genuine weakness is not the choice to spare Inojin. It is the risk that the narrative uses his pain as a short-lived lever for somebody else's development and then rushes past his experience.

Jura's advantage is control, not just a greater pool of chakra

Himawari's offensive display is astonishing. Contemporary coverage describes physical strength, an expanding tail-like silhouette and a Tailed Beast Bomb. These are the visual and mechanical signs readers have historically associated with Kurama's terrifying reserves. But the important reversal arrives when Jura grows more deliberate. He is not simply struck harder by an opponent with better emotional reasons to fight. He is able to exploit the gap between a freshly awakened fighter and an enemy who can measure energy use, pace himself and respond calmly. Himawari's rage provides urgency; it does not provide the experience needed to govern so much chakra.

That distinction improves the power system. Two Blue Vortex has sometimes made its time-skip heroes appear to know exactly what the story requires the moment they arrive. Himawari's emergence follows a more convincing trajectory: an enormous gift carries enormous demands. The antagonist's curiosity about the relationship between her and Kurama also adds uncertainty, but a review should distinguish his observations from an omniscient explanation of the Nine-Tails' new biology. Jura may be perceptive and still wrong about the deeper mechanics. The evidence at this installment supports a power that is unusual and potentially vast, not a definitive ranking of Himawari above every experienced jinchūriki.

Sarada's fight keeps the threat personal in another part of the village

The other confrontation is not an unrelated action break. Hidari focuses on Sarada because the being shaped by Sasuke's chakra has inherited a drive toward the person connected to him. According to published discussion, Konohamaru and Sumire attempt to respond, but Hidari's speed and unconventional movement put Sarada in immediate danger. The encounter illustrates a distinct kind of inherited power. Himawari is endangered by what resides within her; Sarada is endangered by a living distortion of her father's identity. Both are treated as valuable targets before they have any chance to negotiate what those connections should mean.

That structural echo is easy to overlook if the fight is reduced to who could beat whom. The threat to Sarada also puts the manga under pressure to honor her independence. She has spent much of the sequel defending Boruto and promising to rescue Sasuke. Being captured by a Sasuke-shaped enemy could turn her into a symbolic prize. The story needs opportunities for her training, reasoning and choices to affect the conflict, even when a rescue is necessary. Friendship has always involved people saving one another, but repeated interruptions can still restrict a character's agency. The parallel danger to two young women makes this issue especially prominent.

Boruto's rescue demonstrates planning, not omnipresence

The ending makes familiar use of Boruto's Flying Thunder God technique and the small toads that help him follow events. Contemporary reports describe him moving Himawari away from Jura and then responding to Sarada's predicament, cutting through Hidari's attempt to seize her. The rescue works because a prior piece of groundwork pays off. Boruto is not presented as sensing every crisis magically across the village; there is an intermediary network and a marker that make his sudden appearance possible. His intervention is an extension of reconnaissance, preparation and relationships that have continued outside Konoha's official systems.

Yet a rescue is not a full solution. Jura and Hidari are still active, the Thorn Soul problem remains unresolved, and the village is far from accepting Boruto as an ally. The protagonist's physical arrival does not abolish Omnipotence, restore Sasuke or make Himawari safe. That limitation helps preserve tension. At the same time, any recurring pattern of Boruto appearing at the last second must be used with care. If every dangerous scene ends with the same teleportation reveal, audiences may start to feel the supporting cast is simply arranging opportunities for him to look capable. Here the setup is grounded, but the narrative has not escaped that longer-term risk.

Verdict: impressive power with some consequences left unpaid

True Power earns its name not because Himawari defeats Jura, but because the chapter forces readers to separate potential from mastery. Her desire to protect friends is genuine; so is her inexperience. Jura's steadiness makes the imbalance credible, and Shikadai's tactical judgment gives Team 10 a contribution that cannot be measured in chakra output. On the other side of Konoha, the Hidari confrontation connects Sarada's family history directly to the new enemy's appetite. Boruto's use of a prepared communication network gives the rescue a logical basis rather than relying entirely on spectacle.

The reservations matter. Inojin's apparently catastrophic wound is stabilized with a speed that may undercut the previous installment. Sarada again faces the danger of becoming someone for Boruto to save, and the power-system explanations remain provisional. These are meaningful critiques, not reasons to dismiss the chapter. Its best narrative question is whether a gifted child can learn to act responsibly while frightened and furious. This assessment draws on Shueisha's verified volume contents, VIZ's release record and contemporaneous chapter criticism; it is original source-based interpretation, not a claim to have personally read every restricted panel or a fabricated numerical rating.
