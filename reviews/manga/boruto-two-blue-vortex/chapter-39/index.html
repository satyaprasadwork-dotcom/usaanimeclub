Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 39 Review: Sarada Turns the Rescue Into a Team Win

Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 39 review: Sarada's Ohirume gives Boruto the opening for Uzuhiko, while Kawaki challenges Himawari's loyalty.

Source-based review by USA Anime Club Editorial · Research checked November 20, 2026 · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Spoiler notice: this article discusses released installment events described by the sources.

THE SOURCED TAKEAWAY

Sarada’s Ohirume–Uzuhiko combination makes Chapter 39 a meaningful team win, while Himawari and Kawaki’s conflict and Code’s glowing hand keep the larger danger unresolved.

Sarada turns the rescue into a team attack

Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 39, officially titled “Please End Me,” turns the previous chapter’s rescue cliffhanger into a second problem: Boruto has reached Sarada, but Code is still between them and the exit. The chapter was listed by VIZ for November 19, 2026. Its strongest move is making the escape plan evolve into a coordinated answer instead of giving Boruto another solo entrance.

Kashin Koji wants Boruto to take Sarada back to Konoha so she can recover. Sarada argues that they should stop Code first and points to Hidari’s earlier choice to protect her. Koji’s Ten Directions had not shown him that outcome, and he admits the situation is changing faster than his visions can account for. Sarada is not dismissing the danger; she is using new evidence to challenge a plan built on an incomplete forecast.

That disagreement gives the fight a useful character beat. Boruto’s arrival does not erase Sarada’s judgment or make her a passenger in her own rescue. She knows the immediate risk, understands what Hidari’s intervention means, and chooses to act anyway. The choice is reckless in the way Koji fears, but it is also the reason the chapter’s best attack works.

Himawari and Kawaki make the conflict personal

The chapter opens away from the hideout, with Himawari and Kawaki sharing the curry she has made at the Uzumaki home. Their conversation begins with the meal and turns toward Boruto. Himawari continues to believe that the brother she remembers would not commit the crimes attributed to him, and she holds on to the hope that Naruto and Hinata are still alive somewhere.

That faith has a history. In an official Q&A, Mikio Ikemoto explains that Himawari chose to stay in the Uzumaki home because she expects her parents to return, and that she once sought Sarada’s advice because she was worried about Boruto. The scene therefore reads less like a sudden refusal to accept reality and more like a young person protecting the family story she has kept alive through Omnipotence.

Kawaki’s answer makes the warmth of the kitchen uncomfortable. He insists that he will eliminate the Ōtsutsuki, Boruto included, if Momoshiki takes control, then asks Himawari whether she will stand with the Leaf or with her brother. Amado’s scientific ninja tool toad arrives to reinforce the urgency and asks Kawaki for his strength. The scene gives the coming conflict an emotional cost before the next fight can make that choice physical.

Code turns the escape route into a trap

At the hideout, Boruto marks a route through the roots with one of his pins and says the rescue mission is complete. Koji wants him to use it immediately, but Code refuses to let them leave. Claw Marks spread across the space, closing off a clean retreat and forcing the group to keep tracking the threat while they try to preserve an exit.

Koji cannot give them a reliable countdown to Jura’s arrival. That uncertainty matters more than another power ranking: Boruto and Sarada are working against a clock they cannot see. Boruto has to keep the pin in mind, protect Sarada, and respond to Code’s attacks without assuming that Koji’s vision can settle every decision.

Code reads Boruto’s movements as proof that he is willing to exploit Sarada’s position just to reach him. The chapter uses that misreading to set up the reversal. Sarada is still in the fight, and her response turns the moment into an opening Code does not anticipate.

Ohirume gives Uzuhiko the opening it needs

When Code emerges through a Claw Mark, Sarada is already suspended above the ground with her Mangekyō Sharingan active. She uses Ohirume to pull Code toward her while Boruto gets into position below. The attack is built around distance and timing: Sarada moves the target into reach, and Boruto supplies the finishing technique.

The teamwork is more satisfying than a simple sequence of stronger attacks. Ohirume has a clear tactical purpose here, and Sarada’s decision affects the outcome rather than merely adding another visual effect to Boruto’s move. The two do not need a long explanation to coordinate; the paneling makes their roles readable as Code tries to resist the pull.

Boruto lands Rasengan: Uzuhiko once Code is drawn into range. The blue-white spiral against Sarada’s violet gravity effect gives the sequence a distinct visual identity, but its real payoff is structural: the rescue becomes a shared win. Sarada has turned a dangerous fall into the opening Boruto needed.

Uzuhiko stops Code without closing the threat

Code hits the ground and struggles to rise while the planet’s rotation continues to work through him. Boruto explains that the force will not simply disappear, and the scene gives the attack weight without pretending that the larger conflict is over. Code is incapacitated for the moment, but Boruto does not kill him or claim that the danger has been settled.

The closing image keeps that distinction alive. Code’s prosthetic hand begins to glow as he tries to move, a detail the chapter does not explain. It is a tease, not confirmation of a new ability or a successful counter to Uzuhiko. The story leaves room for the hand to matter later while showing that Code remains conscious and furious.

That restraint is useful. Code has spent several chapters being outmaneuvered, so another apparent defeat could make him feel less threatening. The glowing hand and his refusal to let go of revenge do not restore his advantage yet, but they keep the result provisional instead of turning the fight into a clean endpoint.

Jura’s bookstore detour widens the danger

Elsewhere, the chapter checks in on Konoha’s political pressure. Mitsuki is still being questioned by Kobu, who is focused on his own objective, until Shikamaru steps in and takes control of the exchange. The scene is brief, but it keeps the village’s internal struggle moving alongside the rescue rather than letting Boruto’s fight become the only story that matters.

Jura’s conversation with Furuta at a bookstore shifts the tone again. Furuta is explaining animal behavior and the survival value of a cat’s cuteness when Hidari reports that Boruto and Sarada are at the hideout. The casual subject makes Jura’s growing curiosity feel unusual, while the interruption turns that curiosity toward the active conflict.

Furuta still believes the public story that Boruto is a traitor and tells Jura to kill him. Jura answers with a comic thumbs-up and leaves. The joke lands, but it also exposes the danger of misinformation: Jura is now moving with a distorted account of Boruto’s identity, while Shikamaru’s attempt to manage events from inside Konoha remains fragile.

Chapter 39’s best idea is that trust changes the plan

The chapter links its two main threads through competing kinds of certainty. Kawaki is certain that Boruto must die if Momoshiki surfaces; Himawari trusts the brother she knows; Koji’s future sight is useful but incomplete; and Sarada acts on what Hidari has actually done. The story does not present trust as a solution to every danger. It shows how trust changes which risks the characters are willing to take.

The action is the clearest payoff. Sarada’s Ohirume and Boruto’s Uzuhiko work together in a way that gives both characters a necessary role, and the result answers the previous chapter’s rescue setup. The quieter home scene gives that success a cost by making Kawaki’s demand of Himawari explicit rather than leaving the family conflict as distant background.

The pacing is less even when the chapter cuts from the hideout to Mitsuki’s interrogation and then to Jura’s bookstore conversation. Each detour adds a thread, but together they interrupt the fight just as it gains momentum. The chapter’s breadth is valuable; a little more connective space could have made the escalation feel smoother.

Verdict: a strong Sarada chapter with an unresolved finish

“Please End Me” gives Sarada a consequential role in the outcome and makes her partnership with Boruto the chapter’s best sequence. The Ohirume–Uzuhiko combination is easy to follow, emotionally earned by her insistence on confronting Code, and more memorable than another one-sided display of Boruto’s power.

The chapter also advances Himawari and Kawaki’s conflict without forcing a resolution too early. Its final threads leave Code impaired but not finished, Jura newly informed, and the village’s public story still pointed against Boruto. Chapter 39 delivers a satisfying tactical victory while keeping the larger danger open.
