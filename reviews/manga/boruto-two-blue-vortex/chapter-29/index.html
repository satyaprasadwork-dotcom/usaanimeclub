Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 29 Review: Boruto's Byakugan and the Momoshiki Deal Make Every Victory Dangerous

Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 29, Pest Control, analyzed: Boruto's bargain with Momoshiki, Karma and Byakugan, Sumire's upgraded sword, Mamushi's copies, Inojin's tracking technique and Shikadai's new shadow tactics.

Source-based review by USA Anime Club Editorial · Research checked November 15, 2026 · Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Spoiler notice: this article discusses released installment events described by the sources.

THE SOURCED TAKEAWAY

Pest Control finally puts the anti-Mamushi plan into motion and lets the younger team contribute. Boruto's new control over Karma is thrilling precisely because the price he promises Momoshiki could make a successful defense catastrophic later.

The bargain is the chapter's true beginning, not an incidental explanation

Pest Control begins by revisiting the agreement Boruto proposed to Momoshiki. The immediate threat is the sentient Divine Trees, particularly Jura, whose abilities neither Boruto nor Kawaki has yet shown a reliable way to neutralize. Boruto believes he needs the Ōtsutsuki power inside him to continue protecting people. Contemporary criticism describes Momoshiki recognizing that the offer is genuine: Boruto is prepared to surrender his body once the Divine Tree threat has been dealt with, provided Momoshiki cooperates for now and does not interfere while the power is used. The agreement is less like a standard mentor-and-student pact than a contract to postpone a catastrophe by promising another one.

The scene gives Boruto an understandable motivation without making the method comfortable. He has watched multiple ninja suffer for him, saved people whose memories tell them he is a murderer and discovered that even carefully prepared techniques may fail against Jura. A character in that situation might reasonably seek a desperate advantage. But he has also spent years protecting his own consciousness from a being who intends to replace him. Offering that consciousness as payment threatens to undo the very freedom he has been defending. His willingness to sacrifice himself is genuine; its consequences for other people are not exclusively his to decide.

Momoshiki's calculation is equally important. He may obtain control of a powerful body, but Kawaki remains determined to eliminate Ōtsutsuki threats and would be an obvious enemy once the bargain is fulfilled. An agreement between two parties with incompatible ultimate goals is unlikely to remain straightforward. The chapter does not prove that either party can permanently enforce the terms. It establishes that Momoshiki is willing to cooperate for the immediate conflict and that Boruto accepts an enormous future risk.

Karma becomes useful again without becoming safe

The visible effect of the agreement is Boruto's capacity to activate Karma without the immediate struggle over his body that previously made the technique dangerous to use. That is a powerful battlefield development. The series had placed restrictions on his access to one of the most important sources of his strength precisely because Momoshiki could exploit it. Removing the short-term obstacle helps Boruto deal with Mamushi's growing numbers, but it should not be confused with curing the underlying problem. The arrangement makes sense only while both sides believe keeping it is in their interests.

This is a more interesting way to bring back a signature power than simply declaring that training has eliminated the risk of possession. The technique is still entwined with a hostile presence, and the price is explicit. A dangerous advantage can be credible when its use creates a debt the story cannot easily erase. It also changes the reader's response to the fight. Each display of Karma may help protect civilians in the moment while reminding us that Boruto believes he has promised his future autonomy away. That tension is one of the strongest narrative ideas in this phase of the manga.

The concern is whether later installments will keep the bargain's severity visible. If Boruto uses every available ability with no psychological consequence and the promise is quietly forgotten, the scene will become another device for lifting a restriction at the most convenient moment. For now, the character's willingness to accept a cost rather than pretend it does not exist makes the choice compelling.

The right eye reveal should be described accurately, not inflated into a theory

Contemporary reports describe Boruto revealing a Byakugan in his damaged right eye while using the enhanced powers available to him. It is an important visual development because readers have long discussed the status of the special eye shown in the anime. The scene is stronger when the fact is reported narrowly: the manga presents the eye here as a Byakugan connected with the current Momoshiki and Karma situation. That does not automatically resolve every canon question about the Jōgan's depiction in other media, nor does it prove the writers have formally erased the concept from the franchise.

Such caution matters for a fandom in which an image can trigger countless posts announcing a definitive retcon. An honest review can describe the observed eye and its immediate relevance without pretending a chapter provides an editorial statement on future anime continuity. The mystery may be meaningful, but speculation should be labeled as speculation. The bigger story point is personal: Boruto opens an eye associated with the presence inside him at the moment he has agreed to surrender himself later. A power that might save his home is also a reminder that his body is not entirely his own.

The chapter's dramatic treatment works because the reader understands the danger before the display. The eye is not merely an accessory introduced to make the protagonist appear stronger than his rivals. It is attached to a bargain with a known antagonist. The more powerful he looks, the less certain his long-term freedom becomes.

Sumire's sword gets a chance to earn its existence

With Mamushi's copies scattered around Konoha, Boruto uses the weapon Sumire repaired and modified. The preceding installment established that the sword could maintain a specialized chakra-enhanced cutting effect and that unauthorized users would face a chakra-draining defense. In action, the improved weapon gives Boruto a practical means of removing copies rapidly. It is a welcome piece of payoff for the scientific work Sumire performed rather than another instance of the protagonist drawing a completely unexplained tool from his coat.

The blade also draws a throughline between generations. Sasuke's old weapon carries the memory of a mentor currently trapped by the Divine Tree problem; Boruto applies training built during their years together; Sumire provides new scientific capabilities that the original weapon lacked. The combination represents cooperation across different kinds of expertise. It helps the story feel like a world in which shinobi methods and advanced technology evolve together rather than existing in disconnected narrative compartments.

Still, there are limits to what the chapter demonstrates. A blade that can eliminate several Mamushi bodies is not proof that it can instantly defeat Jura or neutralize every Thorn Soul manifestation. A security mechanism that punishes unauthorized wielders might become useful later, but it is not an automatic guarantee that the sword will never be stolen or used against its owner. The most persuasive reading keeps the weapon tied to the particular problem it was designed to address.

Inojin and Shikadai make the defense more than Boruto's spectacle

The younger supporting team contributes meaningfully to the battle. Inojin's new Mind Transmission Formation permits a coordinated response to bodies that ordinary sensing may struggle to detect. Shikadai uses a variation of shadow restraint that allows him to affect multiple enemies and even pull copies from an elevated position. Chocho brings the physical power to follow through, while Himawari uses a measure of Kurama's chakra and speed against attackers. These reported developments are valuable because the defenders are not all required to possess the same kind of strength.

Shikadai's role is particularly satisfying. He has inherited a reputation for strategic thinking, and a shadow technique that changes the movement of several targets fits that reputation better than a generic energy blast. Inojin's ability fills the communications gap that Mamushi's hidden copies create. Chocho's capacity to take advantage of immobilized enemies gives the group a sensible order of operations. Himawari provides frightening potential but still needs guidance about when and where to use it. The combination evokes the tactical traditions of the older Konoha teams without requiring an exact copy of their abilities.

The limitations remain important. Mamushi's numbers continue growing despite several impressive defeats, and the chapter has not fully established that the young fighters can eliminate all copies safely. That is the right kind of suspense for a coordinated defense. Every contribution matters, but the team must use it in a system that adapts as the fight continues.

Kobu's investigation is an enemy of a different kind

While the fighting spreads through Konoha, the inquiry into Boruto's escape continues. Kobu questions Mitsuki and grows more suspicious that somebody with authority supplied the information needed to unlock Boruto's restraints. The investigation is not frivolous under the village's believed history. If an accused killer escaped through internal assistance, security officials would normally be obligated to establish who was involved. The unsettling problem is that the evidence motivating the inquiry is compromised by Omnipotence even as the procedures remain formally legitimate.

The collision between emergency defense and political accountability is becoming harder to ignore. Shikamaru cannot tell everyone what he knows without risking his ability to coordinate the battle, yet an investigation that ignores rules would set a troubling precedent for a leader with secret contacts. The story is strongest when it recognizes both obligations instead of treating every skeptic as a nuisance who deserves humiliation. Kobu's pressure creates a distinct challenge that neither Boruto's Byakugan nor Kawaki's firepower can solve. The next installment needs to show whether Shikamaru can keep people safe without allowing a desperate strategist to decide that inconvenient officials are expendable.

Verdict: a successful fight that makes the future more frightening

Pest Control earns its momentum by paying off several setup chapters. Boruto uses Karma and the improved sword, Inojin's sensory network becomes relevant in the field, and Shikadai's shadow tactics let the younger defenders operate as a genuine team. Mamushi's multiplication creates a problem that cannot be solved by one powerful attack alone. The chapter is also a rare example of a protagonist gaining visible strength through an agreement whose moral price is worse than the tactical problem it temporarily solves.

The main reservation concerns how easily the story may be able to forget that price. Momoshiki's cooperation is alarming, not reassuring, and the current Byakugan reveal should not be inflated into unsupported claims about every future manifestation of Boruto's eye. The political conflict also risks becoming repetitive if the investigators never learn anything meaningful. For now the chapter succeeds by making a village defense feel collaborative while giving the hero a private countdown toward an unthinkable sacrifice.
