Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 28 Review: The Best New Power in Chapter 28 Is a Network, Not a Sword

Boruto: Two Blue Vortex Chapter 28, New Powers, analyzed: Inojin's Mind Transmission Formation, Sumire's enhancements to Sasuke's blade, Shikamaru's team coordination and Boruto's first clashes with Mamushi.

Source-based review by USA Anime Club Editorial · Research checked November 15, 2026 · Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Spoiler notice: this article discusses released installment events described by the sources.

THE SOURCED TAKEAWAY

New Powers is more convincing when it treats science, sensing and teamwork as the heroes' real upgrades. Inojin and Sumire finally contribute tools with distinct limits, although the speed of their breakthroughs and the enemy's shifting behavior deserve scrutiny.

Inojin's technique is an answer to the right problem

New Powers begins with Inojin demonstrating the technique Koji asked him to learn. The accomplishment is notable because the preceding chapter only gave him a narrow training window. Contemporary accounts identify the result as Mind Transmission Formation, a method that turns selected allies into receivers in a network capable of identifying subtle vibrations that normal chakra-based sensing may miss. Mamushi's numerous bodies are dangerous partly because they can conceal their chakra signatures. A strong punch cannot defeat an enemy whose location is unknown. A network that tells the defenders where the threat is moving can make the difference between a coordinated defense and a village-wide series of ambushes.

This gives Inojin a role that belongs to his own abilities instead of simply making him the person Himawari cares about. His Yamanaka heritage is associated with mental communication, but the new formation draws on that expertise in a way that supports the entire team. It is a refreshing expression of Naruto's old preference for interlocking strengths. Shikadai can direct people, Chocho can apply force, and Himawari can deliver devastating chakra attacks. Inojin's contribution enables those abilities to be used against an opponent who might otherwise evade them. The chapter's most valuable change is that he becomes a participant in defending the village rather than a reason for someone else to awaken power.

His achievement also creates an important demand on the writing: show why the technique works. The explanation about sensing vibrations is an accessible starting point, but readers need to understand which targets can be identified, how many allies can serve as receivers and what happens when Inojin loses concentration. The idea becomes compelling when it has limits that an intelligent enemy might exploit. Otherwise it will become a new way for the author to know every location the plot requires.

Learning a future technique in one day risks making growth feel borrowed

Boruto is impressed by the speed of Inojin's progress, and the result makes sense in the context of Koji's unusual access to possible futures. Knowing that a version of Inojin might someday discover a technique can save time spent searching for the basic concept. Yet there is a difference between knowing a procedure and mastering it in a real fight. The account of a sudden breakthrough risks treating training as the transfer of information rather than the accumulation of skill. Naruto's older generations learned techniques through failed experiments, individual variations and hard-earned experience. A future notebook should not automatically eliminate that process.

The chapter offers a partial answer: Inojin's motivation is unusually strong and the technique remains demanding to maintain. It requires attention and coordinated receivers; it does not grant the same scale of communication that Ino demonstrated during the Fourth Great Ninja War. Those limits make his rapid success more acceptable. He has learned something specific for a specific mission rather than becoming instantly superior to every experienced Yamanaka. An honest appraisal should recognize both the clever premise and the convenience. The technique will feel properly earned if Inojin's decisions in the following battle matter as much as the instructions he was handed.

His conversation with Boruto also adds warmth. The older brother's concern about Inojin hurting Himawari is playful, but the humor cannot erase Omnipotence's cruelty. Inojin remembers Boruto as somebody dangerous to the Uzumaki family, while Boruto knows they are speaking about his own sister. The same two boys can cooperate against a threat and remain separated by a distorted account of their relationship. A small exchange becomes emotionally meaningful because the knowledge between them is so unequal.

Sumire's sword upgrade is good science-fiction when it has a failsafe

Sumire returns Sasuke's sword after adapting the technical notes Boruto supplied. Contemporary coverage describes improvements in its sharpness and ability to maintain the Hagoromo technique with less active concentration from the wielder. The most interesting addition is a mechanism that recognizes the chakra of selected authorized users and drains chakra when somebody else attempts to use the weapon. This is a cybersecurity-like principle translated into ninja equipment: rather than trusting that a valuable tool will never be stolen, the design assumes theft is possible and punishes unauthorized use.

This is a meaningful job for Sumire. Her research experience matters in a way that cannot be replaced by merely encouraging the protagonist. It also ties the time-skip story back to Sasuke through a physical object the mentor carried. Boruto's sword is not important only because it signals that he has adopted a certain aesthetic; it represents a legacy that can be protected and transformed through scientific work. Sumire's contribution makes that inheritance a collaboration involving several people, including the person trying to save Sasuke. The effect is more satisfying than assigning her another scene of quiet jealousy.

The problem again concerns information delivered by Koji. If the design comes from a future Sumire who had already made a discovery, the present Sumire has been given somebody else's breakthrough—albeit from a version of herself. The chapter needs to allow her actual experimentation, judgment and accountability to matter. A future document can contain a plan; it should not automatically guarantee that an untested device will work under extreme combat conditions. The undisclosed material used in the sword is also a reminder that the full technical explanation remains incomplete. A critic should not confidently supply components the chapter does not identify.

The battle plan asks the village to trust information it cannot openly credit

Shikamaru coordinates the defense through the developing communication network and attempts to distribute fighters across sectors of Konoha. The decision fits the threat. Mamushi's bodies do not merely line up in a field for a single all-out attack; they can disperse and make the question of detection central. A system that tells each team where to intervene gives even less powerful shinobi a useful assignment. The village's defenses begin to resemble a working organization rather than a stage on which only Boruto and Kawaki are permitted to matter.

Yet this coordination occurs while Shikamaru is under political suspicion for apparently assisting Boruto. The same information that helps defend civilians could expose the officials who have been secretly cooperating with the fugitive. Shikamaru has to act as the responsible leader of a village that believes the wrong history, while preparing for a threat that most of his superiors cannot understand. It is an unusually specific kind of pressure for a battle manga. The enemy's bodies may be multiplying outside, but the village's ability to respond is threatened from inside by a dispute over what counts as reliable evidence.

The ethical tension should not be solved by portraying the investigators as senseless fools. Their concern about the Hokage's hidden contacts is understandable under the world they remember. What makes the system dangerous is its inability to examine that remembered world. A more nuanced story would permit security officials to discover contradictions and choose what to do with them, rather than requiring Shikamaru to conceal every useful fact indefinitely.

Mamushi's arrival tests the logic of the advance briefing

Boruto begins confronting the scattered copies, and the chapter moves toward the action promised in Koji's earlier briefing. Contemporary accounts describe Mamushi's bodies attacking in Konoha, including the use of Fire Style that sets buildings alight. This is noteworthy because the previous explanation suggested that instinctive fear of Jura might constrain how much damage the clones would inflict on the village. The apparent contradiction is either evidence that Koji's estimate had limits or a small break in the consistency of the rules. The story will be more convincing if it acknowledges the difference rather than treating every prior warning as accurate when convenient.

The event also illustrates why containment is a different goal from merely defeating enemies. A city contains homes, civilians and infrastructure vulnerable to even accidental destruction. The fighters cannot simply fire the largest attack available whenever a clone appears. They need to assess where the flames are spreading and where bystanders might be trapped. The chapter's turn toward a dangerous urban environment gives the numerical threat weight. A hundred enemies would be frightening even if each were individually weak because each could begin a separate emergency.

Boruto's confrontation shows that his physical skill is still exceptional, but the story has wisely resisted treating his sword as sufficient. He may dispatch the copies he sees and still fail to prevent another one from moving elsewhere. Inojin's new detection method and Sumire's modified weapon are therefore not decorative side projects. They are attempts to answer the logistical problem that a single extraordinary fighter cannot solve by himself.

The Karma reveal is a promising threat to Boruto's hard-won self-control

The chapter's later action draws attention to Boruto's capacity to use Karma again, after his prior conflict with Momoshiki left him wary of the consequences. At this point readers know he has been considering an arrangement with the Ōtsutsuki presence inside him. The story has not yet fully established that this bargain will be safe or permanently binding. A new battle in which Karma becomes useful may immediately create pressure to keep using a power that could erode the user's autonomy. The stakes are personal: Boruto is trying to save a village whose collective memory treats him as an enemy, and the method of saving it may threaten the only life he still controls.

That gives the confrontation more purpose than another surprise transformation. Sumire can improve a sword, Inojin can coordinate senses and Boruto can employ a dangerous energy source, but each capacity carries limits and responsibility. The chapter's title is most effective if all those changes remain different tools in the same defense. An endless series of upgrades would merely reset the power scale. A collection of specialized, imperfect abilities can make the battle feel like a collaborative problem whose solution must be earned.

Verdict: specialized tools make a stronger ensemble

New Powers is a productive installment because it gives supporting characters something concrete to contribute. Inojin's Mind Transmission Formation attacks Mamushi's main tactical advantage, while Sumire's redesigned sword makes her research relevant to the combat without pretending she must become a front-line brawler. Shikamaru's sector coordination gives those advances a place in the larger defense. Boruto remains strong, but the problem requires more than his strength. That is a welcome correction to a sequel often criticized for leaving older and younger supporting characters waiting for the protagonist to arrive.

The weaknesses are the convenience of rapid training from future knowledge, unexplained details of the sword's technology and potential inconsistency between Koji's predictions about Mamushi and the harm his copies already cause. The chapter also places pressure on the story to explain the real consequences of Boruto's use of Karma. Its best promise is not that everyone suddenly possesses an unbeatable technique. It is that teamwork may matter again when the individual powers have well-defined constraints.
