Research transparency

Research, sources and editorial review

What our published guides offer, what we have not done, and how readers can challenge a claim.

What counts as original value here?

We aim to provide a usable process rather than a collection of slogans: decision checklists, hypothetical cost examples clearly labeled as such, accessibility considerations, and pointers to primary sources. The convention budget worksheet adds an interactive example of how readers can adapt the calculations to their circumstances.

What we have not verified

Publication is not the same as independent fact verification. The existing fifteen guides were initially assembled as editorial explainers and still require a documented, claim-by-claim source review. We have not conducted original interviews, measured streaming availability across platforms, attended named events or verified specific vendor prices on behalf of readers. Automated checks catch metadata and broken internal links, not false claims.

Our priority review process

  1. Identify factual assertions and separate them from opinion and clearly hypothetical illustrations.
  2. Locate original, authoritative evidence. For licensing and legal details, point to the U.S. Copyright Office or the relevant rights holder rather than assuming a fan forum is authoritative.
  3. Verify time-sensitive facts such as streaming catalogs, event policies and fees with current official pages.
  4. Correct or qualify unsupported statements, document the source and the review date.
  5. Publish substantive changes with accurate revision dates and update the guide's source links.

Corrections and reader questions

Send the article URL, the passage and a supporting source to contact@usaanimeclub.com. We cannot guarantee a response until the mailbox is confirmed operational. The repository's editorial review register tracks outstanding reviews without claiming completed testing.

Disclosure

Some guides were drafted or edited using AI assistance. They do not impersonate named experts and are not presented as personal first-hand experiences. Any future sponsored placements or affiliate relationships should be labeled. Advertising approval and editorial quality are different matters: neither code validation nor a monetization decision proves a page's accuracy.

External sources

Useful official starting points include the U.S. Copyright Office, the Federal Trade Commission consumer resources, the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, and each event organizer or service's own current website. Please verify current rules directly.
