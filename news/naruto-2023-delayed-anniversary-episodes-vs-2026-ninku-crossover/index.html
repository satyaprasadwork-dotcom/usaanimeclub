Naruto's Delayed Anniversary Episodes vs Naruto × Ninkū: Are They the Same Project?

Two different Naruto announcements each mention four episodes. Compare the 2023 postponed anniversary animation with the distinct 2026 Naruto x Ninkū crossover.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026 at 3:36 PM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

Naruto's 20th-anniversary anime was announced as four new episodes for September 2023, then postponed on August 29 to improve quality. A separate Naruto × Ninkū four-episode crossover was introduced at New York Comic Con on October 10, 2026 with a different explicit premise and two-creator supervision. The official announcements do not identify the crossover as a renamed airing of the delayed episodes.

Source record: August 29, 2023 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Are Naruto's delayed 2023 anniversary episodes and Naruto × Ninkū the same four episodes?

The official sources describe a postponed 2023 anniversary production and a newly announced 2026 Naruto × Ninkū crossover. They have not established that the two are identical.

Why people are confusing two four-part Naruto projects

Two unrelated dates in Naruto's recent production history can look suspiciously similar in a search snippet. In 2023, the anime committee announced four completely new episodes for the original television series' twentieth anniversary, and later postponed that plan. In October 2026, the franchise announced Naruto × Ninkū as a newly produced four-episode crossover. Both involve familiar young Naruto-era characters and both use the number four, but the official announcements do not say they are the same production.

That distinction is the key answer to the question. It is fair to acknowledge that fans may ask whether one project was repurposed into the other. What is not supported is a categorical assertion about internal assets, budgets or production reuse. No primary record examined identifies the 2026 collaboration as the revised release of the older 2023 anniversary series. Treating them separately is the most accurate reading of the available evidence.

The original anniversary plan in July 2023

The official July 23, 2023 Naruto notice introduced a four-week broadcast beginning September 3 on TV Tokyo-affiliated Japanese channels. Its teaser visual showed Naruto, Sasuke, Sakura and Kakashi from the pre-Shippuden Team 7 period. The announcement also described planned anniversary music: FLOW's newly recorded version of GO!!! and a cover of the familiar Viva☆Rock song. This was marketed as new animation honoring the original anime's twentieth anniversary.

Those concrete details are not interchangeable with a later crossover trailer. The original promotion did not present Fūsuke or Ninkū as story elements, and its scheduling information belonged to September 2023. Anyone finding that older official article today should notice the appended postponement warning, rather than treating the initial day and hour as a still-valid future airing plan.

The August postponement was explicit

On August 29, 2023, the Naruto anime production committee announced that the four-week anniversary series would not air on its originally planned date. It said additional time would be used to improve quality and promised to issue a revised broadcast schedule once finalized. The statement also described replacement programming: selected Boruto episodes would occupy the planned television slot. That is a clear historical postponement record.

The notice did not set a new premiere day or announce that the anniversary project had become a crossover with another franchise. A postponed broadcast is not identical to a cancelled production, but neither can it be reported as completed or released without later confirmation. As of the November 2026 records checked here, this primary statement remains the basis for distinguishing the delayed 2023 initiative from new projects announced years afterward.

The 2026 Naruto × Ninkū announcement is more specific

The October 2026 New York Comic Con event unveiled an original Naruto × Ninkū crossover anime with a named story concept. Naruto, Sasuke and Sakura receive a mission from Kakashi, Naruto serves as temporary leader, and the group wanders into another world where Fūsuke appears. Both Masashi Kishimoto and Ninkū creator Koji Kiriyama oversee the story. The official franchise has created a dedicated project page describing these roles and the premise.

This is a different package of information from the 2023 anniversary tease. It has an explicit second franchise, a creative collaboration and a narrative introduction. The production team also identified staff members associated with Naruto and Ninkū animation. The 2026 statement does not, however, provide a confirmed broadcast date or a US streaming platform. Both projects thus remain distinct in description while having different unresolved schedule questions.

Could the studios share work behind the scenes?

Fans may reasonably wonder whether animation prepared for a 2023 anniversary celebration was later recycled into a 2026 crossover, particularly because the recognizable young Team 7 appears in both promotions. That would be an interesting production question, but the public announcements do not establish it. Sharing a studio, character designer or franchise identity is not documentary evidence that scenes, storyboards or episode masters have been repurposed.

An answer would require a direct statement by the production committee or staff about the relationship between the projects. Without it, the safest formulation is that the two have not been officially identified as identical or as a rebranding. A responsible entertainment publication should not convert coincidental episode counts into a confident behind-the-scenes narrative, even when speculation has spread widely.

A straightforward evidence comparison for fans

The 2023 project has a known purpose: celebrating the original television anime's twentieth anniversary with four weeks of new programming. Its last definite schedule event in the cited record is an August postponement. The 2026 project has a known story: a four-episode crossover of Naruto and Ninkū created with both manga authors' supervision. Its announcement was at New York Comic Con and its specific premiere timing was not revealed.

These are separate fields, and keeping them separate removes most confusion. Neither statement announces an English dub release on Netflix. Neither gives a basis for calling the entire Boruto Part 2 continuation a four-episode special. Likewise, a later Project Rasengan page listing Ninkū does not automatically update the deferred 2023 schedule. A comparison article works precisely because it resists collapsing every Naruto animation headline into one imagined release.

How to recognize each teaser correctly

A promotional image containing young Team 7 is not enough to identify the project. For the delayed anniversary work, compare the 2023 official teaser and the music announcement tied to its former September slot. For Naruto × Ninkū, check the later official project site for the crossover logo, Fūsuke's appearance and the other-world mission introduction. A date and source link should accompany any reposted clip used as evidence.

The same care applies to headlines about Naruto's live-action movie or the Shadow Rising manga. They are different media projects, however similar their character portraits might look. A short caption identifying the precise source, year and production title helps prevent a reader from believing a nostalgic teaser announces a brand-new streaming premiere.

What would settle the outstanding question

The decisive evidence would be a clear rights-holder notice specifying whether the 2023 anniversary episodes remain separately planned, have been cancelled or have been incorporated into another named work. Absent that notice, the correct status for the original production is an announced postponement with no replacement broadcast date established here. A newer Ninkū premiere announcement would update only that crossover unless it explicitly explained the older series.

For US fans, the practical conclusion is that Naruto has two documented four-episode announcements from different years, not one confirmed release calendar. The first was postponed after a September 2023 plan; the second is a distinct 2026 creative crossover announcement. USA Anime Club should maintain different links and timestamps for both rather than suggesting the shared episode total proves they are secretly the same thing.
