The Beginning After the End: Differences Between the Novel and Comic Editions

The Beginning After the End original web novel is complete and relaunched as revised ebooks; its Tapas comic has a distinct episode and hiatus schedule.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

TurtleMe's The Beginning After the End novel concluded its serial run and moved into Aethon Books' revised ebook program. The separate Tapas comic announced a pause after Episode 259 in October 2026, so novel completion should not be confused with the adaptation's status.

Source record: October 26, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Which edition continues the story you are currently reading?

Compare the novel and Tapas comic as separate adaptations; their chapter numbers and publication progress do not correspond one-to-one.

One story, several separately published versions

The Beginning After the End began as a prose fantasy by TurtleMe, the pen name of author Tae Ha Lee, and later became a popular illustrated comic. It also has an anime adaptation. Those versions share the reincarnation premise but should not be treated as interchangeable products. A reader can buy an ebook, read a comic episode or watch an animated scene, each with a different publisher record, pacing and release schedule. The correct starting point depends on which version the reader actually wants.

The premise follows King Grey, a ruler whose military strength and wealth did not resolve his isolation. Reborn in a world of magic, he receives a different opportunity to build a life and address choices from his past. The broad setup applies across the franchise, but that does not guarantee that an episode of one medium corresponds to a chapter with the same number in another. Editorial comparisons should identify the edition rather than assume the numbering systems align.

The original novel has completed its serial run

TurtleMe and publishing partner Aethon Books have confirmed that The Beginning After the End's long-running prose serial concluded. In an August 2025 announcement, Aethon said the weekly web novel had ended and described plans to relaunch revised ebooks with new covers and additional professional editing. The author subsequently wrote that the Tapas novel would leave the platform in February 2026 as the completed story transitioned to its book editions. Those are explicit status statements, not conclusions inferred from a quiet episode feed.

This is important for US readers searching for the latest novel chapter. A completed serial is not missing its next weekly installment simply because the old publishing platform has stopped showing new chapters. It has shifted to a different distribution stage. A reader returning after several years should check which ebook edition is available rather than search for an unauthorized continuing copy that claims the author is still posting the original novel indefinitely.

Revised ebooks and legitimate purchasing routes

Aethon's publisher announcement describes revamped ebook editions intended for Kindle and Kindle Unlimited. TurtleMe's own website provides dedicated book and audiobook links for the novel and identifies the comic and anime as separate adaptations. This gives readers two directly attributable routes to establish the author's intended editions. It does not mean every third-party storefront showing the title is an authorized distributor, and it does not guarantee identical availability in every country.

The revised program involves fresh covers and an editing pass, so an older ebook cover and a newer one can belong to related but not identical presentations of the same volume. Buyers should inspect the listed author, volume order, edition and language rather than conclude that all duplicates are counterfeit. The publisher also said previously purchased editions would remain in a reader's library. That promise concerns existing legitimate purchases; it is not a guarantee about unlicensed files or unrelated subscription services.

The Tapas comic is a separate serialized adaptation

Tapas identifies the illustrated The Beginning After the End as an action-fantasy comic credited to TurtleMe and associated production contributors. Its series page notes that the comic is based on the original novel. The current production information gives adaptation, script, artwork and editorial credits, making clear that converting prose into a scrolling comic is a collaborative endeavor. A comic episode is not simply a screenshot of a novel chapter; visual pacing and page composition introduce different editorial choices.

The same Tapas page announced a hiatus after Episode 259 was scheduled for Thursday, October 29, 2026. The October 26 notice stated that the extent and duration of the break had not yet been determined. It would therefore be misleading to say the comic is completed merely because the original novel finished, or to publish a firm return date without a later statement. A promised future comic season and a confirmed novel ending are two different publication statuses.

How adaptations change the reading experience

Prose can describe King Grey's thoughts, motives and inner narration directly. An illustrated adaptation must express many of those elements through framing, visual design, dialogue and the order in which panels appear on a screen. Tapas's scrolling interface shapes pacing differently from ebook pages. That is a structural difference, not proof that one version is inherently better or more faithful. A thoughtful comparison requires identifying particular scenes and the editions being examined.

The anime introduces another format with performance, music, animation and episode-length constraints. A television episode need not end where a comic installment or prose chapter ends. Search results sometimes promise a definitive chapter mapping based only on episode numbers; such claims should be checked against the published work. Readers who want to avoid spoilers can follow one medium in order before searching for correlations with later material. The creator's official site separates the novel, comic and anime precisely because they are different access paths.

Why the October comic hiatus matters

The comic's October 2026 hiatus notice changes how a reader should understand the word updates on its landing page. A generic Thursday schedule can describe the title's usual publication pattern while a dated hiatus message explains why another regular episode may not arrive. The two pieces of interface text do not necessarily contradict each other. The dated announcement gives the stronger evidence for the temporary status after Episode 259.

Readers also should not confuse the comic pause with a halt to the entire franchise. Completed novels can remain available as books, and existing animation can still be watched under its own distribution arrangements. A publisher may make new announcements about any of those media at different times. Unless a named rights holder says otherwise, a hiatus in the illustrated series does not establish a cancellation of all related projects or a specific deadline for another medium.

Where to begin and what to verify before paying

A new reader primarily interested in text should start at TurtleMe's novel page and follow the identified ebook or audiobook options. Someone seeking illustrated storytelling should open the official Tapas comic page, inspect the first episode and its current access rules, and note the hiatus message before assuming weekly updates. A viewer interested in animation should use an official streaming listing for the TV adaptation rather than equate its catalog with novel ownership.

Different access mechanisms may apply to each version. A purchase of a remastered ebook does not automatically unlock every comic episode on Tapas, and an app's promotional free-reading offer should not be interpreted as perpetual ownership of a prose volume. Similarly, previous purchases and new subscriptions can involve different account conditions. Check the exact publisher and product description, particularly when older websites still reference the removed Tapas prose serial.

What future reporting should monitor

A meaningful update would identify a dated announcement resolving the comic's hiatus, a new author-confirmed book edition or a specific licensing change for the animation. The author and publishers have already supplied important milestones, so another article that simply asserts a new novel chapter is due without evidence would be inaccurate. The proper questions are what format changed, which company announced it, and whether the information affects US readers' current access.

As of the reviewed records, the distinction is straightforward. The prose story has reached completion and has a revised ebook distribution route. The illustrated Tapas comic is a separate adaptation with a publicly announced pause after an October 2026 episode. That combination allows readers to choose a legitimate format and avoid treating the completion of the underlying novel as the end of every adaptation.
