The Apothecary Diaries Season 3: US Episode Schedule, English Dub and Streaming Guide

Where to watch The Apothecary Diaries Season 3 in the US, when the English dub started, how the split-cour schedule works, and what to know before watching.

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Where can US viewers find the current English dub and subtitled Season 3 episodes?

Use Crunchyroll’s episode-level audio selectors. Season 3 launched October 2, 2026 and its English dub began October 16; the next cour is due April 2027.

Overview

If you've finished the first two seasons of The Apothecary Diaries and are wondering whether to start the third in Japanese or wait for the English dub, you no longer need to make that choice. Season 3 is already underway, and Crunchyroll has begun releasing its English-language version. The larger question is how the season's unusual schedule works, particularly for viewers who want to watch everything in one uninterrupted run.

Season 3 premiered on October 2, 2026. It is a split-cour production: the first part began in October, while the second is scheduled for April 2027. Crunchyroll is streaming the series as it airs. The English dub began releasing weekly on Friday, October 16, 2026, two weeks after the Japanese-language premiere. These dates are from official announcements, not a projected calendar built from previous seasons.

Where can US viewers watch Season 3?

Crunchyroll is the confirmed streaming home for Season 3 in the United States. Its seasonal lineup and individual English-dub announcement establish availability. Search for The Apothecary Diaries in the Crunchyroll app or website and choose Season 3; the service may place the dubbed and subtitled versions under separate language options. What appears by default can depend on a viewer's preferred audio settings.

A search result that says an episode exists does not necessarily mean that the audio option you want is available yet. Before assuming a dub is delayed, open the episode information, review available audio tracks and compare the episode number with the subtitled listing. If the title is absent from a different service, don't assume this is a technical glitch: licensing can vary, and past-season availability does not guarantee that new installments arrive everywhere at the same time.

For a U.S. audience, it is worth making a distinction between the date an episode becomes available and the hour it goes live. Official releases have confirmed the Friday English-dub cadence, but streaming service calendars can shift for holidays, special programming or production changes. An article that permanently promises one exact Eastern or Pacific time without rechecking a current official schedule could mislead readers.

How does the split-cour schedule work?

A cour is roughly a three-month block of Japanese television programming. A split-cour season spreads its parts over different broadcast blocks instead of airing continuously. For this season, that means an October 2026 starting point and an April 2027 return, not a separate fourth season. The pause is part of the announced broadcast plan; it is not, by itself, evidence of a cancellation or unexpected delay.

That difference matters to fans catching up over winter. Someone starting in November can watch the episodes that have arrived, then choose whether to keep following weekly or stop at the cour break and return when April's episodes begin. The latter approach may suit anyone who dislikes stopping midway through a long mystery. The former gives viewers time to think through clues, compare possible explanations and enjoy the story's weekly conversation.

Don't count the second cour as a new season simply because a platform places it in a separate playlist. Streaming libraries sometimes organize parts, seasons and languages differently from Japanese production announcements. The reliable identifier here is the official Season 3 announcement.

Is the English dub out yet?

Yes. Crunchyroll announced that weekly Season 3 English-dub episodes began on October 16. That makes a significant difference from an announcement that merely promises a dub sometime in the future. English-speaking viewers can already begin watching, while remembering that the dub normally trails the subtitled release schedule.

The safest approach is to follow the dubbed track's actual episode list, not infer its progress from the number of subtitled episodes. A two-week launch gap is not a guarantee that every later episode will maintain an identical gap throughout the run. When publication-day changes matter, treat the individual episode release information as the final reference.

A dub is also not an inferior way to experience the story. The Apothecary Diaries depends on unusually precise exchanges: a casual remark can conceal a political calculation, and Maomao's dry responses often carry more than one meaning. A viewer concentrating on English dialogue may follow those interactions differently from someone reading subtitles. Neither choice makes the viewer more or less serious about the series.

What should you remember before Season 3?

The foundation is the relationship between Maomao, a sharp-eyed young woman whose knowledge of medicine and poisons repeatedly proves useful, and Jinshi, whose position in the imperial court makes their interactions unusually complicated. The series isn't only a romance, and it isn't a conventional case-of-the-week detective show. Court hierarchy determines who can safely say what, while gossip and small domestic decisions can become matters of political survival.

Coming into the third season, a refresher should focus less on memorizing every secondary character and more on three questions: what Maomao knows that the powerful people around her don't, what Jinshi can or cannot disclose about himself, and why seemingly minor medical incidents may have wider implications. Those recurring tensions are a better preparation than a giant spoiler-heavy recap.

If you are a newcomer, begin with Season 1 instead of jumping into the current episodes. Many mysteries are accessible individually, but the emotional stakes accumulate gradually. A first-time viewer who starts with Season 3 will recognize that Maomao is clever; they may miss why her reluctance to draw attention is so important.

How to avoid spoilers while following the season

The light novels, manga adaptations and anime do not always move through the story at the same speed. A discussion labeled “Season 3 theory” can still reveal future material from a published book. Look for discussions explicitly restricted to the anime, and avoid searching for an unexplained character's full name until you've watched the newest episode.

When reading comments about Maomao and Jinshi, separate a viewer's interpretation from what a scene explicitly establishes. Their story encourages close reading, but a fan's confident theory is still a theory. That distinction is particularly important for a website reporting on an ongoing series: the answer to “what might happen” should not be presented as confirmed news.

Is there also a movie?

Yes. A separate film, The Apothecary Diaries: The Late Lady's Treasure, has been announced for a December 11, 2026 theatrical release in Japan. Crunchyroll announced plans for worldwide theatrical distribution. That is not the same thing as announcing a specific U.S. opening date, theater list or ticket sale. Those details should be covered in a dedicated film guide and updated only after distribution information is published.

For now, treat the third season and the movie as two distinct viewing developments. You do not need to resolve hypothetical continuity questions before the film has been released and its relationship to the television material can be described accurately.

The bottom line for US fans

Season 3 began October 2, the English dub launched October 16, and the announced second cour starts in April 2027. Crunchyroll is the confirmed U.S. streaming destination. If you want weekly discussion, start now; if you prefer longer uninterrupted mysteries, the split-cour structure makes waiting a reasonable choice. What you should not do is confuse the planned break with an unannounced delay or mistake the film's Japanese date for a confirmed U.S. premiere.
