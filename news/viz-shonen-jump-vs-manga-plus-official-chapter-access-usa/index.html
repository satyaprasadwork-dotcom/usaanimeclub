VIZ vs. MANGA Plus: How Official Chapter Access Works in the United States

Compare VIZ Shonen Jump, VIZ Manga and Shueisha's MANGA Plus for free chapters, subscriptions, First Read Free, legal access and US reading choices.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

THE STORY AT A GLANCE

VIZ's Shonen Jump and VIZ Manga services operate distinct English-language libraries, while MANGA Plus by SHUEISHA supplies an international official chapter platform. Free recent chapters, paid archives, app-only First Read Free and purchased volumes each have different access terms.

Three services that readers often combine under one name

VIZ's Shonen Jump service, VIZ Manga and MANGA Plus by SHUEISHA are all legitimate manga-reading options, but they are not three identical subscriptions. Shonen Jump emphasizes English chapters from its associated manga library, including well-known action series. VIZ Manga covers another catalog with creators and genres ranging from Frieren to Rumiko Takahashi's works. MANGA Plus is Shueisha's international platform for serialized manga, including works whose new installments are released around the same time as in Japan.

The first useful step is to search for the actual series before paying for anything. One Piece and Kagurabachi have official Shonen Jump directories, while another publisher title may be part of VIZ Manga or available through a different licensed service. Seeing a familiar VIZ logo on a paperback does not mean that the entire franchise is included in both VIZ app subscriptions. The catalogs, territorial rights and forms of access need to be checked independently.

How VIZ's Shonen Jump membership works

VIZ's official apps page describes a Shonen Jump subscription priced at $3.99 per month at the time of this review and a digital library of more than 20,000 chapters. The company advertises free recent chapters for selected titles, alongside membership access to a wider back catalog. Its own FAQ also describes reading the latest chapters of many series on the same day as Japan. Those are useful US reading features, not a promise that every title from every manga publisher is available.

For example, VIZ's One Piece directory displays Chapter 1198 dated November 8, 2026, and its Kagurabachi directory shows Chapter 138 on that date. Those chapter pages are appropriate for readers keeping up with ongoing stories. The purchase of a separate printed or digital volume is still distinct from a membership. Before subscribing, compare the number of chapters you need to catch up on and whether the particular title is in the subscription library.

Why VIZ Manga is a separate subscription

VIZ also offers a product called VIZ Manga, with its own mobile application and a digital library of more than 10,000 chapters. The company's apps page lists a $1.99 monthly subscription price and examples such as Frieren: Beyond Journey's End, Komi Can't Communicate, Nana and several works by Rumiko Takahashi. This is not simply the Shonen Jump subscription at a lower price; VIZ gives the products different catalogs and marketing identities.

A common mistake is to see a low advertised monthly fee and assume it includes every VIZ publication. Someone seeking a particular Shonen Jump title may need to look in Shonen Jump instead, while another reader interested in romance, horror or older works could find VIZ Manga more relevant. The official VIZ FAQ details regional eligibility for each product, so people traveling or sharing recommendations internationally should verify their location as well as the title. Prices and catalogs can be revised; check the app before subscribing.

MANGA Plus and its First Read Free rules

MANGA Plus by SHUEISHA is an official service operated by the Japanese manga publisher. Its website and app make selected ongoing series available internationally, often emphasizing new chapters and an opening selection of installments. The official First Read Free explanation says that eligible ongoing English-language titles can have chapters read once without charge in the mobile app. That is a specific benefit with limits; it does not mean every existing chapter is permanently free to reread.

The rules explain that the free reading opportunity can end when the chapter viewer is closed, that some chapters or titles have exceptions, and that rereading can require subscription or other access. Crucially, the same First Read Free facility is not available on the ordinary MANGA Plus website; it is an app feature. A reader who only uses a desktop browser can therefore encounter a different experience from someone with the official mobile application, even while looking at the same series.

What free means across the platforms

Free can describe several different products: a selection of introductory chapters, the most recent installments, a single app-based read of an older chapter, or a temporary promotion. These are not equivalent permissions. A reader who opens one chapter without paying has not necessarily bought the file, acquired permanent rereading rights or gained offline access. The distinction becomes clear when a story has hundreds of installments and a reader wants to revisit earlier material after catching up.

VIZ's Shonen Jump FAQ describes a rotating selection of recent free chapters and broader archive access through membership. MANGA Plus's documentation explains eligible First Read Free chapters and potential subscription requirements. Which route is better depends on the specific title, reader's device, region and desire to reread. A price-only comparison would omit those important practical differences. Before relying on a free offer, check its chapter-level label and any account conditions presented by that platform.

Print books and purchased digital volumes are another category

Neither subscription should be confused with owning a printed manga volume. VIZ sells English paperbacks through bookstores and provides digital purchase options for selected collected editions. Those purchases have edition identifiers, prices and format terms different from a rolling chapter library. The One Piece Volume 113 product record, for example, has a November 10, 2026 English release date and its own ISBN. That is not the same thing as the newer Chapter 1198 entry in the official Shonen Jump list.

The same principle applies to series such as Dandadan and Ichi the Witch. A December collected volume can be scheduled weeks after digital chapters with higher numbers are available. Readers who want to support creators and maintain a physical collection may choose books; readers prioritizing rapid access to ongoing installments may choose a licensed chapter service. A reasonable guide helps readers identify these goals instead of insisting that subscription is universally cheaper or that physical purchases automatically include digital-access codes.

A practical decision path for US readers

First search the series title on the official VIZ Shonen Jump, VIZ Manga and MANGA Plus pages to establish which services actually carry it in the United States. Second, decide whether you need the newest installment, the full archive, one-time access or a lasting purchased edition. Third, inspect the specific chapter labels and subscription terms in the app or website you intend to use. This sequence prevents paying for the wrong catalog or assuming a one-time benefit is permanent.

Finally, distinguish authorized sites from unofficial mirrors. VIZ and Shueisha publish official translation and access information, whereas an unlicensed copy can have missing pages, altered chapter numbering or no dependable rights status. The concrete November 2026 comparison is that Shonen Jump and VIZ Manga advertise separate US subscriptions and MANGA Plus offers a different set of international reading tools, including an app-only First Read Free feature. Check current conditions before purchase, since prices, rights and product interfaces can change.
