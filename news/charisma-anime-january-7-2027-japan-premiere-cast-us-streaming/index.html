Charisma Anime: January 7 Premiere, Cast and US Availability Questions

The official Charisma anime website names a January 7, 2027 Japanese TV premiere and reveals its cast and Pierrot Films staff. A US streaming service is not yet identified.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

The Charisma television anime begins January 7, 2027 on 28 TBS-network stations in Japan, with a late-night Thursday broadcast. Its official website names the cast and Pierrot Films production staff but does not establish US streaming access.

Source record: November 1, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Does a Japan premiere announcement also name a US streaming service?

Separate the Japanese broadcaster and date from any explicitly announced North American license; an unannounced stream is not confirmed.

A dated Japanese premiere, not yet a US release

The official Charisma anime project announced a January 7, 2027 television debut on Japan's TBS network of 28 affiliated stations. The stated weekly broadcast time is Thursday at 11:56 p.m. Japan Standard Time. Those details are precise for the Japanese airing arrangement, but they do not automatically supply a United States streaming platform, a US-local release hour or an English-dub plan.

This is a common point of confusion with late-night anime. Japanese television schedules and North American simulcast services follow different licensing and delivery arrangements. An English-language news story about a Japanese announcement is not itself a rights acquisition. Before advertising the title as available in the US on a specific app, a distributor must publish a territory statement or otherwise make its official episode listing accessible.

The November 1 announcement also included a new promotional video and additional cast details. A trailer can show that the production has reached a public promotional stage, yet a preview uploaded to an official account is not the same thing as a weekly overseas release contract. American readers can confidently record January 7 as the Japanese premiere and leave the US streaming question open.

What Charisma is about

Charisma began as a media-mix project organized around a group of seven distinctive men sharing a house. The television anime draws on that fictional setting, with personalities built around exaggerated versions of personal charisma. The official project description emphasizes characters trying to understand and develop their individual presence while living together, a setup that lends itself to ensemble comedy and unusual interpersonal clashes.

A premise involving several defined personalities can be difficult to describe without reducing the entire series to a list of labels. The house is a shared location in which the residents' habits collide, and the characters' ideas about themselves may be as important as their interactions with visitors. The premise supports an introduction to the property's tone, but it does not establish particular episode endings or a comprehensive adaptation of every earlier audio and multimedia entry.

That background helps newcomers distinguish Charisma from another show with a similar ensemble or a music-focused title. The adaptation is connected to an existing franchise with its own characters and project history; it is not an original American streaming series announced merely because English sites reported the Japanese trailer. The title and official anime domain are the best identifiers to use when looking for actual announcements.

Main cast and the newly added roles

The official cast listing includes Yuki Ono as Fumiya Ito, Masahiro Yamanaka as Rikai Kusanagi, Katsumi Fukuhara as Iori Motohashi, Kenta Hosoda as Kei Sarukawa, Saku Hyuga as Ohse Minato, Genki Okawa as Terra and Tomohisa Hashizume as Amahiko Tendo. These names define the principal group and make clear that the anime has publicly credited performers rather than only a provisional concept trailer.

The November news release additionally identifies Shinnosuke Tachibana as Soemon Nakagami and Tomohiro Ono as Shuu Torahime. The two roles are distinct from the established seven residents; an additional performer credit is not a reason to rename or renumber the original main group. Readers should verify character spellings against the official site because romanization may vary across English-language summaries.

Voice casting can be relevant to readers who follow particular Japanese performers or know the source project's audio work. It does not, however, identify any English dub cast. A Japanese voice actor appearing on the official production roster and a future American dubbing announcement are separate pieces of news. Until a US distributor provides the latter, reporting familiar English performer names as confirmed replacements would be unsupported.

The production staff behind the adaptation

The Charisma anime's staff credits name Joji Furuta as director and Shu Matsubara for series composition and the screenplay, with Pierrot Films responsible for animation production. Haru Watanabe handles character designs, while the original character concepts are credited to Ebimo. Music-related production credits involve EVIL LINE RECORDS and Gesshoku Kaigi. These are named roles that readers can check without relying on speculative animation-industry accounts.

A director and screenwriter influence how an existing project is translated to television, but their past work does not provide an advance review of the new series. The combination of ensemble characters, heightened personalities and an established multimedia identity raises creative questions about pacing, performance and what material is adapted. Answers require either a detailed official production explanation or the actual episodes, not assumptions imported from another show in the staff's filmography.

The project's origin is credited to Dazed CO., LTD. and Shu Matsubara. That credit matters because it connects the screen adaptation with the property rather than treating the character concepts as suddenly created for the January broadcast. Attribution is also useful when distinguishing an original character designer from a television character-design role; those positions can belong to different contributors on the same adaptation.

A November short anime is a separate programming item

Before the January 2027 television launch, the franchise announced a short-form Charisma segment beginning November 4, 2026 within TBS's Yoru no Brunch programming. That is a separate Japanese broadcast entry from the January regular television premiere. Anyone looking up the franchise on a schedule site may find both dates and incorrectly conclude that the main season changed its launch day.

The distinction is practical rather than technical trivia. A promotional or short-format program can introduce characters or raise interest while the full television project has a later debut. It may use some of the same branding and voices, but it should not automatically be counted as the first regular episode of the January series. The official announcements label the formats differently, and a reliable guide should do the same.

For US readers, neither a short segment on Japanese television nor an online teaser establishes a North American distributor. The fact that a clip can be viewed from another country is not proof that full episodes will be licensed there. Maintaining separate entries for the November short material and January television launch avoids both misleading watch-order advice and invented streaming promises.

What to check before January 7

The first unresolved matter is whether a rights holder names an American streaming partner. A useful announcement would identify the service, the covered territories and whether the release follows Japan's broadcast. Second comes language access, including any confirmed English subtitles or dubbing. Third is the actual episode-page schedule, which can differ from the Japanese network's late-night local time.

Viewers also may wish to know the number of episodes, their average runtime and whether any portions of the earlier media-mix project are recommended background viewing. Those details should be taken from official production information, not assumed from the existence of a weekly Thursday slot. A program appearing on 28 Japanese stations tells readers about domestic carriage, not how many episodes an international subscriber will receive.

As of the November source check, the confirmed details are the January 7, 2027 Japanese premiere at 11:56 p.m. on TBS affiliates, a defined main and additional Japanese cast, and Pierrot Films' credited production team. The absence of a named US service in the official announcement should remain visible until a separate distributor statement provides the missing information. That is the fairest way to cover a promising new adaptation without overselling its availability.
