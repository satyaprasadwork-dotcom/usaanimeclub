Pokémon Adventures Red and Blue Returns in a New US Deluxe Hardcover

VIZ released Pokémon Adventures: Red and Blue Deluxe Edition Vol. 1 on November 10, 2026, as a 476-page hardcover with an all-ages rating.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

VIZ confirms a November 10, 2026 release for the 476-page deluxe hardcover of Pokémon Adventures: Red and Blue, Volume 1, written by Hidenori Kusaka and illustrated by Mato.

Source record: November 10, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What material does this hardcover actually collect?

Confirm the contents and format from the publisher rather than assuming the hardcover matches a single earlier paperback.

What the deluxe edition contains on paper

VIZ Media has listed Pokémon Adventures: Red and Blue, Deluxe Edition, Vol. 1 with a November 10, 2026 release date. It is a hardcover of 476 pages with ISBN 978-1-9747-6938-4, in a 6¼-by-9-inch format and with an All Ages rating. The publisher credits Hidenori Kusaka for the story and Mato for the art.

The new format is the key news: this is an edition of established Pokémon manga material, not a claim that a new Pokémon Adventures storyline debuted this week. Collectors deciding between formats should pay attention to the binding, dimensions, contents and edition identifier, rather than relying on the shared series title alone.

The story introduced to new readers

According to VIZ's synopsis, Red approaches Pokémon training as friendship as much as competition, while encountering Bulbasaur, Poliwhirl and a stubborn Pikachu. His rivalry with Blue develops alongside a threat from Team Rocket. These are the narrative landmarks VIZ chooses to introduce the book; the product page does not require readers to follow a separate streaming release.

The deluxe reissue is potentially useful for readers who want a sturdier collector format or who are beginning with the original Red and Blue-era adventures. That is an editorial observation about formats, not a claim that the new volume changes the underlying plot.

US availability and purchasing checks

VIZ's listing names the hardcover format and an official release date; retailer availability, regional pricing and delivery times may vary. The publisher's suggested US price is not necessarily the amount charged everywhere. Match the ISBN before purchasing, particularly when older paperback compilations or other Pokémon manga arcs appear in search results.

Nothing about this print announcement establishes a new anime season, game remake or international release outside the publisher's listed market. The confirmed development is the English hardcover edition, with a documented format, publication date and ISBN for US readers.

What makes this hardcover a distinct edition

The headline distinction is format rather than a new narrative. VIZ's Red and Blue Deluxe Edition, Volume 1 is listed as a 476-page hardcover, with a larger 6¼-by-9-inch trim size and an All Ages rating. Those are concrete differences that someone choosing between older paperback collections and a deluxe volume can inspect. They do not establish that the script has been rewritten, additional adventures inserted or art recolored. A special-edition label can be a useful collecting signal, but the product description should decide which specific features a buyer expects.

VIZ names the story's writer, Hidenori Kusaka, and the illustrator, Mato, rather than presenting the book as a generic Nintendo product. This matters because Pokémon Adventures is a comics adaptation with its own storytelling choices. Readers may know Red, Blue and Team Rocket from games or animation, but they should not assume every character's background or every encounter follows those other versions scene for scene. A familiar franchise title identifies the fictional world; author and artist credits identify the particular publication being sold here.

The official November 10, 2026 date identifies when the English-language hardcover was scheduled for release. Readers comparing listings should match ISBN 978-1-9747-6938-4 as well as the format. Retail pages sometimes blend different Pokémon Adventures titles together, particularly when merchandise recommendations include game guides, character encyclopedias and other manga arcs. If the cover or dimensions do not match the publisher entry, checking the catalog number is more reliable than assuming a widely recognized franchise logo is enough to identify the edition.

What first-time readers should understand about the story

VIZ's synopsis introduces Red as a trainer who wants companionship with his Pokémon rather than treating them only as tools for winning contests. Bulbasaur and Poliwhirl figure into that approach, while Pikachu initially presents a greater challenge. As competition with Blue develops, the two rivals face a common threat associated with Team Rocket. This provides a workable entry premise without spoiling later volumes or claiming that the hardcover contains a newly written plot specifically for this release.

The appeal of starting at the Red and Blue era is largely structural: newcomers can begin with foundational characters rather than dropping into the middle of a much later arc. There is still a difference between recognizing a game location and understanding the comic's particular characterization. Someone who remembers the original games may enjoy noticing similarities, but readers are better served by treating the manga as its own telling. We are not promising a one-to-one conversion of game quests or an exact match with the long-running animated series.

The All Ages label is helpful as a publisher classification, but it should not be mistaken for a substitute for a household's own reading preferences. Some families care about conflict intensity, vocabulary and how quickly a younger reader can follow a multi-character scene. A publisher's age label provides broad guidance, not a clinical recommendation for a particular child. Library browsing, a legitimate preview when available, and a conversation about interests may be more useful than assuming that anything related to Pokémon is identical in tone across video games, animated episodes and print comics.

A collector's comparison that goes beyond the cover

Think about the physical reading experience before ordering. A 476-page hardcover is a more substantial single volume than a short monthly comic, and the listed trim size gives readers a sense of how it might fit on a shelf. Some collectors prefer a hardcover because of its binding and presentation; others prefer lightweight paperbacks for commuting or lending to friends. Neither preference proves the new edition is inherently better for every reader. The useful distinction is that VIZ states the material dimensions and format in a way a buyer can compare with an edition already owned.

The publisher's store page displayed a suggested price of $29.99 when checked. That figure is a reference price, not a guarantee about an individual shop, tax, shipping or discounts. It also says actual prices may vary. If a retailer offers an unusually inexpensive copy, verify whether it is a new hardcover, a used copy, an older paperback or even an unrelated volume with a similar title. A marketplace photograph of a spine can be less reliable than the full edition name and ISBN printed in the listing.

Finally, separate the franchise's different release calendars. This news is about an official English manga edition dated November 10. It does not announce a new Pokémon game, television season or theatrical film. It also does not reveal how many additional deluxe books will be published unless the rights holder confirms them. A transparent edition-focused article gives collectors enough information to find the intended book and decide whether it complements their current shelf. The VIZ source is available below for readers who want to verify the publisher's page count, credits and release entry themselves.
