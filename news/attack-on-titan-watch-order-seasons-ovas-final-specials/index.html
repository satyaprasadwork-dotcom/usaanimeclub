Attack on Titan Watch Order: Seasons, OVAs, and the Final Specials

A beginner-friendly Attack on Titan viewing order that keeps the main story clear and places the optional OADs without spoilers.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 at 2:24 PM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

The simplest first watch is Season 1, Season 2, Season 3 Parts 1 and 2, then The Final Season Parts 1 and 2 followed by The Final Chapters special episodes. The eight OADs are optional; No Regrets and Lost Girls are easiest to place after the relevant characters and events have been introduced. The Last Attack is a compilation-film option, not a replacement episode required between seasons.

Source record: March 1, 2023 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What is the simplest Attack on Titan watch order?

Watch the TV seasons in release order through The Final Chapters. The eight OADs are optional extras; place character-focused entries after their relevant introductions to avoid spoilers.

The clean first-watch order

For the least confusing first watch, follow the release order of the main television story: Attack on Titan Season 1, Season 2, Season 3 Part 1, Season 3 Part 2, The Final Season Part 1, The Final Season Part 2, and then The Final Chapters. Streaming services sometimes divide these into different season numbers or label the finale as a special, so follow the installment names if an app's rows look inconsistent.

You do not need to stop for every OAD or compilation film. The eight OADs add character backstories and side episodes, but the main plot is complete without them. If you want the most spoiler-safe route, watch the television story first and return to the optional episodes when the featured characters have appeared. This order preserves the surprises that the original broadcast sequence was designed to deliver.

The main series in order

Season 1 introduces the walls, the military branches, and the mystery that drives the first major story. Season 2 narrows its focus around a smaller group of characters and reveals information that changes how earlier scenes read. Season 3 expands the conflict beyond the immediate threat and is commonly split into two cours. Watch its Part 1 before Part 2 even when a service groups them under one season heading.

The Final Season also uses several labeled parts and a pair of feature-length concluding specials, often called The Final Chapters. Those are the continuation and ending of the television story; they are not optional recap movies. Some regional services have repackaged the finale into episodes, while others preserve the long-special presentation. Check the actual runtime and title in your service so you do not accidentally queue a recap edit thinking it is the conclusion.

Spoiler-aware optional OAD placement

The OAD archive is where watch-order lists can become overcomplicated. The most beginner-friendly approach is to finish a season before exploring its side material. Ilse's Notebook has a natural slot after Season 1 because it adds history to the Scout world without asking you to understand later revelations. The lighter side episodes can also be saved for a break between seasons.

No Regrets focuses on Levi's past, so it is more satisfying after you have met him in the main story. Lost Girls includes Annie-centered material and should not be watched before the anime has established her importance. The table suggests broad spoiler-safe placements, not strict chronology. If an OAD appears in the streaming app before the main season, resist the temptation to start with the app's first row; catalog order is not always the best narrative order.

Where The Last Attack fits

Attack on Titan: The Last Attack is a compilation film presentation of the concluding material, created for theatrical viewing. It can be a convenient way to experience the ending on a large screen, but it is not a new season that belongs between The Final Season and The Final Chapters. Crunchyroll announced a US and Canada theatrical event in 2026; that announcement concerns a cinema window and does not prove a later streaming date.

If you have already watched the final specials, the film can offer a polished rewatch. If you have not, use the service that carries the main series and confirm whether the theatrical film is available in your region before planning around it. Avoid watching a finale compilation early because its marketing description may make it sound like an independent movie while it contains the conclusion of the core story.

Chronological order is not the best beginner route

Several OADs take place earlier in the timeline than their release position, which invites fans to construct a chronological marathon. That can be fun after a first watch, but it is risky for newcomers because a character's backstory may reveal information the main series withholds. Attack on Titan relies on the viewer learning what the characters know at the moment the story chooses to share it.

Release order keeps those discoveries intact and is easy to follow across platforms. Chronology answers “when did this event happen?” rather than “when should a first-time viewer learn it?” If you are revisiting the show, an alternate timeline order can illuminate foreshadowing and character history. For the first viewing, trust the main seasons and add OADs later.

A practical US streaming checklist

Before starting, search the full title and verify that the service lists the original Japanese audio and the English dub for the seasons you intend to watch. US availability can be split by season and platform, especially for a long-running show with older licensing agreements. A listing for Season 4 does not guarantee that Seasons 1 through 3 are included in the same subscription.

Keep the OADs and The Last Attack separate from your must-watch list. If your service has all main seasons, save the end specials until you have finished The Final Season Part 2. If it does not, use the official franchise portal to identify the correct installment names and look for legitimate digital or physical releases. Region-specific catalogs change, so verify the actual US page on the day you plan to watch.

Which route should you choose?

For a first timer: watch the main seasons and final specials in release order, then decide whether you want optional character stories. For a rewatch: add the OADs at the spoiler-safe points in the table, or build a chronology after you already know the central twists. For a movie night: The Last Attack is an ending recap-and-finale presentation, not a shortcut to understanding the whole series.

The strongest advice is to avoid treating a watch-order list as a test. You do not need every bonus episode to understand the central story, and you do not need to memorize military ranks before pressing play. Use the simple order first; expand it only if you want more time with the characters. That keeps the series' carefully timed reveals intact and the viewing plan manageable.

The short version

Watch Season 1, Season 2, Season 3 Parts 1 and 2, The Final Season Parts 1 and 2, and The Final Chapters. The OADs are bonus material with spoiler-sensitive placement, and The Last Attack is a later compilation-film option. That is the whole first-watch plan.

If a service uses unusual season labels, match the title wording and story order rather than guessing from a number. Keep the official portal and Crunchyroll guide close for the optional archive. You can always add side stories later; the main television route already gives you the intended beginning, middle, and end.
