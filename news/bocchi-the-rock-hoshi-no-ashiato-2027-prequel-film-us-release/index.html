BOCCHI THE ROCK! Prequel Film: What Is Known About the 2027 Release?

Aniplex confirms BOCCHI THE ROCK! Hoshi no Ashiato for Japan in 2027, focusing on Seika and Nijika Ijichi. US theaters and streaming remain unannounced.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

Aniplex's November 1 announcement sets BOCCHI THE ROCK! Hoshi no Ashiato for a Japanese 2027 theatrical release. The prequel follows the Ijichi sisters, with Shin Wakabayashi directing at CloverWorks; there is no confirmed US theatrical date in that announcement.

Source record: November 1, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Has an overseas theatrical date been confirmed?

Check the production announcement for the Japan premiere and look for a separate US distributor notice before planning a screening.

An official prequel film, not a renamed TV season

Aniplex announced a BOCCHI THE ROCK! theatrical prequel titled Hoshi no Ashiato, or Starry Footprints, on November 1, 2026. The official Japanese news item gives the film a 2027 release year, publishes an early trailer and introduces the project with cast and staff comments. The language of the announcement is about a movie, not a retitled installment of the television series or a compilation of previously aired episodes.

The production centers on Seika Ijichi and her younger sister Nijika before the formation of Kessoku Band. This focus is meaningful because the television series is usually introduced through Hitori Gotoh's struggle to connect with others through music. The new film turns to an earlier family history linked with the band and the live-house environment around it. It asks viewers to consider the circumstances that made the later ensemble possible.

A release year has a different degree of precision from a release date. Aniplex's announcement establishes a Japanese 2027 theatrical window, but it does not identify an American opening weekend, select US cinemas or promise immediate international streaming. A search result displaying a specific US calendar date without a named distributor should therefore be treated as unverified until a proper domestic release notice appears.

The Ijichi sisters and the story's source

The prequel adapts material associated with the bonus story at the end of the fifth volume of Aki Hamazi's BOCCHI THE ROCK! manga. Its emphasis is on Seika and Nijika, their relationship and events preceding the band's familiar formation. This is not an indication that the movie will revisit every episode of the television series from a different camera angle. The original material offers a more tightly focused perspective on the sisters' earlier lives.

Seika is an important presence in the live-house setting, while Nijika becomes a central member of the band. Placing their history in the foreground can add context to later relationships without requiring the production to invent a new band or introduce an unrelated lead. The official premise points toward earlier circumstances and a shared dream, but it does not authorize a detailed retelling of unseen scenes or the outcome of the full film.

Readers looking to prepare can start with the television series for character context or consult the authorized manga edition for the original bonus material, taking care not to confuse volume numbering between language editions. The announcement's connection to volume five describes an adaptation source, not a guarantee that every page will be reproduced in the same order. Film storytelling has its own pacing, editing and visual choices.

Confirmed filmmakers and performers

The staff announcement names Shin Wakabayashi as director, Erika Yoshida as screenwriter and Yukiko Horiguchi as character designer. CloverWorks is the animation production studio, a fact also recorded on the studio's own official works page. The first announced cast includes Maaya Uchida as Seika Ijichi and Sayumi Suzushiro as Nijika Ijichi. These credits provide a concrete basis for describing the film's creative team.

A list of staff can suggest areas worth watching, such as the treatment of performance scenes or the visual character acting between the sisters. It is not evidence of the finished film's critical quality. A director's previous work and a studio's reputation cannot replace a review based on the actual completed feature. Likewise, a returning performer does not establish every additional appearance or an entire cast list for scenes not shown in the first announcement.

The credited creators also help distinguish this prequel from the separate TV continuation. News sites sometimes group all projects under the franchise name, obscuring which team is attached to which production. A report that identifies Hoshi no Ashiato by its specific title, the named director and its film format gives readers a better way to verify that a circulating promotional image belongs to this announcement.

What Japanese ticket information does not tell US fans

The Aniplex news item mentions an initial Japanese advance-ticket product with newly drawn artwork and a limited sticker bonus. It gives Japanese purchase details tied to local cinema distribution. That merchandise and ticket information is evidence of promotional activity in Japan; it should not be reinterpreted as an American preorder, a US theater reservation or a fixed worldwide premiere date.

The distinction is particularly important for collectors. An unfamiliar retail page may display prices in yen, a Japanese-language theater list or booking rules valid only in Japan. Buying a Japanese promotional ticket does not secure entry to a hypothetical United States screening. Anyone seeking a US showtime should wait for a distributor or exhibitor announcement naming American theaters and dates.

A US theatrical rollout could eventually involve subtitled and dubbed screenings, a limited engagement or a broader run, but none of those formats can be guaranteed from the November release-year announcement. Similarly, a film beginning in Japan in 2027 is not proof that it will reach an American subscription service that same month or even in the same calendar year. Those are separate licensing decisions.

How this differs from the announced second TV season

Aniplex's film reveal arrives alongside an already announced BOCCHI THE ROCK! television second season that remains in production. Both projects can exist simultaneously without one replacing the other. The prequel explores earlier history involving the Ijichi sisters; the TV continuation follows the broader animated series. Confusing them leads to reports that falsely treat a movie poster as evidence of a Season 2 broadcast date.

The creation of a prequel does not fix the release order of all future media. A studio can develop different projects in parallel, and each may receive its own trailer, staff update or opening window. Readers should track titles separately and record the format that the official account uses. The presence of a movie announcement should not be taken to mean a second season has been delayed unless the production company expressly reports such a change.

For someone new to the franchise, the film may eventually offer background that enriches the television story, but that does not establish that it is a required first viewing. Prequels often make emotional use of relationships already familiar to fans. Without viewing the finished work, the safest recommendation is to recognize the timeline and source material rather than declare a definitive viewing order based on assumptions.

The next reliable US release signals

A useful next update would identify an authorized North American distributor, a theatrical opening date or a confirmed international festival or licensing arrangement. Each would answer a different question. A distributor provides evidence that rights are being exercised in the territory; an opening date tells viewers when to plan; a theater list shows whether the film will actually play near them. A social-media countdown with no named company cannot replace these details.

Other worthwhile confirmations include the film's final runtime, available subtitle and dub formats, and eventual home-video or streaming plans. These may be disclosed at different times. The current official source supplies a Japanese release year and production credits, not a universal promise about all later distribution. Keeping that boundary visible makes the report more accurate as new announcements arrive.

For now, the facts are clear: BOCCHI THE ROCK! Hoshi no Ashiato is a genuine prequel film, scheduled for Japan in 2027, built around the Ijichi sisters and produced by CloverWorks with named creative staff. It is distinct from the separately planned second television season. American readers looking for tickets or streams should wait for country-specific evidence rather than assume the Japanese announcement already covers their region.
