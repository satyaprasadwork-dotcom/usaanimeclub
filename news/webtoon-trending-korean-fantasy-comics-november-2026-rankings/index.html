WEBTOON's Trending Korean Fantasy Comics: What the Current Rankings Show

A dated look at WEBTOON's November 2026 trending chart, featuring Regression of the Close Combat Mage, The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower and Eleceed.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

WEBTOON's checked English trending list mixes romance, fantasy and action. Regression of the Close Combat Mage ranked third, The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower fifth and Eleceed eighteenth in the captured list. Rankings are changing platform signals, not monthly Google searches.

Source record: November 14, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Is a listed position current, or preserved from an earlier snapshot?

Check WEBTOON's live ranking and category on the day you visit; do not treat a changing list as a permanent popularity measure.

What the ranking can actually tell readers

WEBTOON's English-language rankings provide a snapshot of which comics the platform currently displays as trending, popular or associated with other discovery categories. They are useful for noticing titles that might otherwise be hard to find, but they are not a direct measurement of all Korean webcomic readership. At the November 14, 2026 check, the visible trending list included Regression of the Close Combat Mage in third place, The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower fifth, and Eleceed eighteenth. These are positions in one displayed platform list, not permanent rankings.

The list also contains romance and drama entries that are not primarily fantasy-action comics. The Perfect Contract and A Child Who Looks Like Me appear at the very top of the checked trending chart. Excluding those entries when quoting the ranking could mislead readers into believing the fantasy titles were first and second overall. A more precise description is that several Korean-inspired fantasy and action works appeared within a broader cross-genre list.

Ranking positions can change quickly as the platform recalculates engagement and presents different views. An editorial article should state the date and name of the list rather than refer to it as a permanent official top ten. The useful story is how a reader can interpret the chart and select a legitimately accessible comic, not an invented league table of universal audience preferences.

Regression of the Close Combat Mage's prominent position

The English WEBTOON list placed Regression of the Close Combat Mage third in the snapshot examined. Its title signals familiar fantasy conventions involving a return to an earlier point and combat-focused magical ability. That may help explain why it appeals to readers interested in progression stories, but a ranking position alone cannot establish specific plot events or the quality of the work. An accurate preview should consult the series' own publisher synopsis before summarizing its narrative.

For a reader deciding whether to start it, the category tag and episode page are more actionable than the chart number. The direct listing shows the available installments, a content rating and whatever reading options the platform currently supports. Those details can change independently of trending rank. Someone who notices the series at number three today may still be able to find the official title after it leaves the visible top thirty.

A high rank also does not prove an anime adaptation is underway. Popularity is sometimes followed by licensing and multimedia projects, but these are separate decisions requiring their own announcements. A title's position in a digital-comic chart should not be converted into a statement about television rights, print contracts or a release window for an unannounced adaptation.

The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower in the same list

The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower appeared fifth on the checked chart, under WEBTOON's Action category. That placement helps readers discover another established fantasy-action property alongside newer and less familiar names. Its presence does not make it a substitute for a separately published ranking of fantasy series only; the source list mixes genres and can order works according to metrics the publisher does not completely disclose on the public page.

A title with an extensive back catalog can appeal to someone who wants a large body of material to read, whereas a newly launched series may be easier to start without a major time commitment. The appropriate comparison is therefore not simply which comic has the larger following but how much official English reading material is accessible, whether the work is ongoing and which access terms apply. The individual series page, rather than the ranking overview, provides those answers.

It is also worth separating interest in a premise from proof of active current growth. A longstanding hit can appear in the trending list following a newly discussed chapter, promotion or increased discovery. Without an official explanation for the ranking algorithm, a writer should not claim to know why this precise title moved. Describing the observed position is defensible; attributing a cause without evidence is not.

Eleceed and other action titles outside the top ten

Eleceed appeared eighteenth in the checked WEBTOON trending list. Its own official English directory separately shows Episode 411 dated November 9, 2026, with a Tuesday update label. Linking a platform rank to a current episode record provides more value than simply celebrating the rank: readers can find the work, confirm it is still updating and identify the authorized English translation. Those are independent observations from two pages of the same publisher.

Other action titles, including Hero Killer, also appeared within the wider top-thirty results. Fantasy is not a single uniform style: some works focus on magic systems and regression, while others center on superpowered confrontations or heroic identities. A reader drawn to one might not enjoy the other. Genre labels and official series descriptions can refine discovery better than assuming all chart entries share the same tone or audience.

Rankings can also vary between Trending, Popular, Originals and CANVAS views. The site offers multiple navigation choices rather than one universal master list. Comparing positions across tabs without recording the tab name is equivalent to combining different charts into a single result. A sound methodology labels the source list before attaching any interpretation to an individual title's placement.

The Remarried Empress spin-off shows the reach of romance

The Remarried Empress: Spin-off appeared in the lower portion of the checked trending chart, at number twenty-eight. That is notable because the title is connected with an established fantasy-romance property, while WEBTOON currently categorizes the spin-off under Romance. It highlights that interest in Korean webcomics is not limited to game mechanics or action-heavy battles. Readers of court drama and relationship stories share the same platform-discovery surface.

The official English spin-off listing credits SUMPUL, HereLee and Alphatart, while the parent The Remarried Empress is marked completed. Those direct records provide important context that the ranking itself does not explain. A new entry in a chart does not reveal whether a series has a fixed weekly schedule, how many episodes are free or whether there will be an English paperback. The title page is still required for those questions.

Including such entries makes a Korean comics trend article more representative. A chart dominated in parts by romance should not be rebranded as solely a fantasy-action chart to fit a headline. A better editorial approach explains which relevant titles are present, acknowledges the larger mix and directs readers toward the current authorized pages.

Why these rankings cannot stand in for Google trends

WEBTOON displays its own engagement indicators, such as cumulative series views, subscriber counts and chart movements. These are platform signals and may reflect repeated reading across many episodes. They do not tell an editor how often Americans searched for a term on Google or Bing during a particular month. A large total view count could have accumulated over several years while a newly released title temporarily trends through increased recent attention.

A high chart position does not prove that a comic has the highest overall sales or the largest global fandom. Geographic markets, translation availability, catalog age and promotion all affect what an English-platform audience sees. Without access to the precise ranking methodology, comparisons should remain descriptive rather than mathematical claims about market share. Google Trends and verified search-console data would address different questions.

This distinction matters for SEO strategy at an independent publication. A platform ranking is a good lead for investigating whether readers need a source-based guide, but it is not sufficient to publish invented search-volume numbers or claim a title is the most searched Korean comic. Original reporting can add context through official series status, creator credits and reading restrictions instead.

A useful way to turn the snapshot into a reading list

Pick a few titles from the observed chart, open each official English WEBTOON page and record the current classification, content rating, episode count and availability status. Note whether the listing is ongoing, completed or on hiatus. That last detail is particularly important because a title can remain highly visible long after a pause; a ranking entry should not be treated as evidence of a new episode.

A reader can compare Regression of the Close Combat Mage and The Advanced Player of the Tutorial Tower as fantasy-action options while looking to Eleceed for a more character-driven supernatural action setup. Someone interested in imperial romance can inspect The Remarried Empress spin-off as a separate work rather than searching only the fantasy top ten. The goal is to make discovery efficient without claiming that rankings measure artistic quality.

The November 14 snapshot confirms that Korean fantasy and action works occupy meaningful places within WEBTOON's wider trending results. Since the chart is dynamic, later articles should record their own dates and avoid silently updating historical numbers. A credible list gives readers navigable sources and explains the difference between visibility, availability and popularity before offering an opinion about what might be worth reading next.
