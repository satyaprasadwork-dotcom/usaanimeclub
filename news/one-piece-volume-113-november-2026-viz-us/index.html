One Piece Volume 113 Reaches US Readers: VIZ Confirms November 10 Release

VIZ Media lists the US paperback of One Piece Vol. 113 for November 10, 2026, including ISBN, format and the Elbaph-focused synopsis.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

THE STORY AT A GLANCE

VIZ lists One Piece Volume 113 as a November 10, 2026 US paperback release: 208 pages, ISBN 978-1-9747-6670-3 and a publisher synopsis focused on the Elbaph conflict.

The confirmed US edition

VIZ Media's official product page dates One Piece, Vol. 113 to November 10, 2026. The paperback is listed at 208 pages with ISBN 978-1-9747-6670-3, under the Shonen Jump imprint and with a Teen age rating. VIZ also lists a digital format, but paperback and digital availability should be checked separately with the relevant retailer or reader service.

That is a straightforward US publication update, not a new anime episode announcement or a claim that Japanese and English volumes are released simultaneously. The printed-volume number matters because One Piece also publishes chapters on a different schedule. A chapter that is available digitally does not automatically mean its collected English volume is on shelves.

What the volume's synopsis signals

VIZ's description places Luffy and Loki in the Elbaph storyline as the Holy Knights of the World Government make a move against the giants' children. That is enough to tell returning readers where this collection sits, but not a complete chapter-by-chapter breakdown. The publisher's description stops at the central conflict; the volume itself supplies the outcome.

For readers who are collecting the series instead of reading weekly, volume 113 offers an official checkpoint with a stable ISBN. It can also be used to distinguish the English paperback from retailer listings for Japanese-language books, digital chapters and future omnibus editions that may use similar artwork.

What US buyers should check

Before placing an order, match the publisher, volume number and ISBN. A listed release date is a publisher target and does not certify inventory at every store, a shipping arrival date or a promotional discount. VIZ's storefront is the primary reference for format and bibliographic information; retailer stock can change independently.

The news value here is the verified English collection reaching its listed publication date. Future anime adaptation coverage, licensing updates and streaming schedules should be reported as separate events rather than inferred from the manga's print calendar.

How a collected volume differs from following One Piece weekly

One Piece exists in several legitimate reading rhythms. Some fans follow individual chapters as they are officially published; others wait for collected paperback volumes so they can read a longer sequence without the pauses between installments. Volume 113 belongs to that second rhythm. VIZ's product information establishes the book's release date and format, but it does not equate a print collection with the latest chapter available on a digital service. Treating those as interchangeable can leave a reader with mistaken expectations about what material a particular purchase contains.

The distinction becomes especially useful for a series that has been running for many years. An English volume number is a reliable way to organize a physical shelf, while a chapter number is a different catalog identifier. Readers comparing a friend's current discussion with a bookstore entry should identify whether the conversation concerns a chapter, a television episode or a collected book. A manga scene being discussed online does not mean it has appeared in the latest official US paperback, and an anime adaptation does not necessarily cover the same material at the same time.

VIZ's 208-page listing gives prospective readers a measurable object: a specific paperback, not an open-ended subscription or bundle. It also helps avoid accidentally selecting a Japanese edition with similar cover art when ordering online. The US ISBN, 978-1-9747-6670-3, is particularly useful because titles and cover images can look alike in international retail catalogs. A quick ISBN comparison is often a more dependable shopping check than a retailer's automated recommendation that pairs several unrelated editions under one series heading.

Reading the Elbaph premise without turning news into spoilers

The official description places Luffy and Loki amid an attack involving the Holy Knights and Elbaph's giant children. That is a focused description of the conflict, not a license to invent the sequence of scenes or identify an outcome. In a heavily discussed adventure series, readers routinely encounter speculation about character motives, alliances and unseen chapters. A publication article should be careful not to translate those conversations into purported facts. Our description stays at the level of the publisher's synopsis and leaves major developments for the book itself.

The setting has narrative weight because giants and island-specific societies are part of One Piece's larger worldbuilding. Readers who have been following the series will recognize why a conflict in Elbaph matters. New readers, however, are likely to benefit more from starting earlier than from purchasing volume 113 in isolation. The issue is not that a high number makes a book inaccessible by definition; it is that recurring characters, running motivations and earlier story arcs normally provide essential context. That recommendation is about reading sequence, not a claim that VIZ requires prior-volume purchases.

The publisher's synopsis also gives readers a useful limit on how much they should expect from promotional copy. It names a threat and introduces questions; it does not verify fan theories about who wins, which character returns or how future installments will resolve the conflict. When comparing reviews, look for whether a writer distinguishes personal interpretation from reported information. The publisher's dated product page confirms the release; questions about the volume's themes require reading and analyzing the actual chapters.

What US collectors can verify before ordering

Start with the bibliographic basics: One Piece, volume 113, Eiichiro Oda credited for story and art, the Shonen Jump imprint, paperback format, 208 pages and the stated November 10, 2026 release. These are facts supplied by VIZ. If a seller lists a conflicting page count or an unfamiliar ISBN, investigate whether it is displaying another territorial edition, a digital product or a placeholder listing. A general cover thumbnail is not enough to resolve that ambiguity, particularly with international editions in the same store catalog.

Next, consider what a publisher release date actually means. It means the English edition has an announced publication date, not that every local bookstore has it physically on hand the same morning. Shipping practices, replenishment and price promotions vary by seller. In an American library, an acquisition record may appear before a branch has the book available for checkout. Readers looking for quick access should compare an official digital option, local library search and reputable bookseller records rather than assuming the print listing is a real-time inventory tool.

Finally, separate buying advice from news. A retailer discount is not proof that a volume has entered the bestseller chart, and a social post showing a large display is not evidence of nationwide distribution. The correct edition can be identified by its title, volume number, publisher, release date and ISBN. It does not claim independent sales data, exclusive interviews with VIZ or a review of every chapter in this collection. Readers can use the linked publisher page to confirm the official product information, then decide whether their preferred reading habit is serialized chapters or the collected English paperback.
