One Piece Box Set 5: What US Collectors Should Check Before Buying

VIZ schedules One Piece Box Set 5 for December 8, 2026. Check the 91–111 volume range, poster, trading-card playsheet, ISBN and buying considerations.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

THE STORY AT A GLANCE

VIZ's One Piece Box Set 5: Wano to Egghead is scheduled for December 8, 2026, containing 21 volumes numbered 91–111, a double-sided color poster and a trading-card-game playsheet. It does not include the separately released Volume 113.

What the fifth English box contains

VIZ's product record names the new collection One Piece Box Set 5: Wano to Egghead, scheduled to release in the United States on December 8, 2026. It includes volumes 91 through 111, making 21 numbered paperback volumes when counted inclusively. The publisher also lists a double-sided color poster and an exclusive paper playsheet using artwork for the One Piece trading card game. Those extras are named parts of the advertised package, not gifts a retailer may arbitrarily add.

The edition has ISBN 978-1-9747-6942-1 and a stated combined length of 4,296 pages. Its publisher page presents a list price of $250 with a caution that actual selling prices may vary. These identifiers are useful because a listing called One Piece box set can refer to four earlier collections, another geographic edition or a seller's custom assortment. A buyer should confirm this particular book range before paying for a large shipment.

The most important volume-number check

The box starts at Volume 91 and ends at Volume 111. It therefore does not start the manga from Chapter 1, replace any of the earlier box sets or extend automatically to Volume 112 or Volume 113. The English Volume 113 has its own November 10, 2026 release record, but it is outside the 91–111 contents listed for Box Set 5. A collector who already owns through Volume 111 might gain little new reading material from buying this set.

That matters because online descriptions frequently shorten the box's subtitle to Wano to Egghead. An arc name suggests a narrative period but is not a substitute for the explicit numerical range. Checking the numbers identifies overlaps with existing shelves and exposes accidental gaps. Someone who owns Volumes 91–100 individually, for instance, would receive duplicates of those volumes if they bought the set for the remaining books. Keeping a written inventory avoids surprise repurchases.

How the bonus items affect the decision

The poster is advertised as double-sided, offering an extra display object for collectors. The playsheet is a paper product bearing One Piece trading-card-game artwork, and should not be treated as a guarantee that the box contains playable trading cards. The official description does not list an exclusive card deck. A marketplace that advertises cards or other merchandise as included should provide separate proof rather than relying on a reader's association between the franchise and its card game.

For someone who already owns the full run, the extras may be the principal attraction. That makes preservation and packaging quality relevant: ask whether a retailer ships the collection as a sealed publisher product, what happens if a corner arrives crushed and whether bonus materials are present inside the box. None of those practical details can be guaranteed from a publisher's standard product photo, especially when the final purchase is fulfilled by a third-party reseller.

Price, shipping and the difference from buying singles

VIZ displays a $250 reference price for the entire set. Dividing the sticker price among 21 included books may help a reader compare the approximate per-volume cost with buying singles, but it ignores shipping, taxes, promotional discounts and the monetary value assigned to the extras. The buyer's real choice is not whether $250 is objectively a bargain; it is whether the offered complete package improves on the cost and convenience of filling the particular gaps in their collection.

Large books can be costly to ship, and the combined page count makes this a substantial physical purchase. Retailers may charge different delivery amounts or restrict returns after opening a shrink-wrapped package. A cheaper listing from an unfamiliar seller can become expensive if it arrives incomplete or requires international return postage. Compare final checkout totals, seller reputation and how damage claims are handled. Price comparisons should refer to actual available listings rather than invented discounts.

Matching this set with the previous four collections

VIZ offers earlier numbered box sets for different stretches of Eiichiro Oda's manga. The fifth collection is intended to follow those products; it is not a reprint of Box Set 4 under new packaging. The subtitle Wano to Egghead places it later in the series, while the actual book numbers define the continuity. Readers assembling a full shelf should check where their existing editions stop and where Volume 91 begins, rather than trusting a generic phrase such as completes the series.

Different English manga formats can introduce additional confusion. Three-in-one omnibuses combine multiple books into larger editions, while standard paperbacks keep the individual volume numbering. A collector who mixes formats may already own some of the same content despite having fewer physical spines. Build a list of underlying volume numbers across all formats before concluding that a new box fills an unexplored section of the story. The purchase decision is about content overlap, not just shelf appearance.

How to verify a legitimate preorder

The official US release date is December 8, 2026, but that does not mean every shop will deliver on exactly that day. A preorder is a commercial arrangement with a particular merchant. Before ordering, match the publisher, product title, 91–111 volume range and ISBN. Compare any promised arrival date and shipping cost against the seller's own policies. Imported products and preassembled bundles may use similar thumbnails while having different contents.

The publisher page's listed dimensions and combined length can help distinguish a boxed set from a single volume or a promotional print, although a retailer may abbreviate technical specifications. Avoid purchasing on the strength of an exaggerated scarcity claim or a listing that cannot identify which editions are included. A substantial collection is better bought with clear refund, replacement and damage terms. Even an officially released item can be temporarily backordered or delayed by a distributor.

Who this collection suits, and who should skip it

Box Set 5 is a practical choice for someone who wants volumes 91–111 as a contiguous group and values the publisher's bonus items. It is a less efficient way to catch up for someone already holding most of those books, or for a new reader who has not obtained Volumes 1–90. Readers whose priority is the latest weekly chapter may get better value from an authorized digital reading service, which follows a different publication schedule from the box.

The central verified information remains the December 8 release, 21 included numbered volumes, ISBN, double-sided poster and paper playsheet. Those facts are enough to ask useful retail questions without promising inventory, special cards, a future discount or the inclusion of Volume 113. Treating the set as a defined physical edition rather than a vague franchise celebration helps US collectors decide whether it genuinely completes part of their library.
