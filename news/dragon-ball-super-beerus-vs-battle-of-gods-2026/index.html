Dragon Ball Super: Beerus vs. Battle of Gods — What Changed in the 2026 Anime?

Is Dragon Ball Super: Beerus a remake, a remaster, or a new story? Here's what Toei confirmed and how it compares with Battle of Gods and the original Super TV arc.

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Is Beerus a completely new Dragon Ball story?

Compare Toei’s stated reworked footage, fresh sound and reconstructed narrative with Battle of Gods and the original Super opening. Avoid describing it as an entirely new sequel.

Overview

A new Dragon Ball title is enough to make old fans ask a surprisingly difficult question: am I being invited to watch a new chapter, or a familiar chapter in a new form? Dragon Ball Super: Beerus makes that distinction unusually important. It revisits the arrival of Beerus, the God of Destruction, but Toei Animation is not presenting it as simply another upload of an old television season.

The enhanced edition started broadcasting in Japan on October 11, 2026, and Crunchyroll began streaming it in October. According to the official production description, it features newly added and revised scenes, re-rendered footage, refreshed sound, newly recorded dialogue and a reconstructed presentation of the narrative. Those are tangible production changes. They do not automatically mean every event in the underlying story has been rewritten.

Three versions of a defining Dragon Ball encounter

The first landmark is Dragon Ball Z: Battle of Gods, the 2013 movie that introduced Beerus to movie audiences. Its central conflict begins with an intimidating visitor who turns out to be less interested in conquering planets than finding a satisfying opponent. Beerus's presence expands the scale of the franchise: Goku's ambition is suddenly being measured against someone for whom the fate of a world can be a casual decision.

The second landmark is the God of Destruction Beerus Saga in the original Dragon Ball Super television anime. That series revisited the story in episodic form. It devoted more time to some material and approached scenes through the different rhythms of a weekly television production. Fans who remember the movie more vividly may already have noticed that a familiar event can feel different depending on which version they watched first.

The third is the 2026 enhanced edition, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus. The official description explicitly frames this as a reconstructed edition of the Super anime, rather than a previously untold post-Super storyline. That is the distinction to keep at the front of the article. People searching “new Dragon Ball anime” may reasonably expect something that follows the Tournament of Power. The Beerus project starts much earlier in the timeline.

What Toei has actually confirmed

Toei describes extensive new cuts and revisions to existing scenes. It also says the original footage has been re-rendered, the soundtrack and effects reworked, dialogue recorded anew, and the narrative reconstructed. In practical terms, a fan could reasonably expect adjustments to composition, shot transitions, the sound of an encounter and the pace at which an episode makes its points. The especially important phrase is “reconstructed narrative”: this refers to how the familiar events are presented, not a guarantee that the series changes their ultimate outcome.

The official English-language website highlights Beerus's awakening, his search for the Super Saiyan God and the danger to Earth as core story elements. Goku, Vegeta, Whis and Bulma remain central to this version. The story's familiar foundations haven't been hidden or replaced by a mysterious new protagonist.

A frame-by-frame claim such as “this punch is twelve seconds longer” would require a documented side-by-side viewing comparison. Without one, that belongs in a follow-up visual analysis, not in a supposedly verified news piece. The same rule applies to claims about new dialogue changing continuity; a promotional description of “newly recorded” dialogue does not necessarily establish that the words have changed.

Is this a remake or a remaster?

Neither label is quite complete. A remaster normally suggests that existing audiovisual material has been improved for presentation. A remake suggests a more extensive re-creation. Beerus incorporates elements people associate with both: it updates original footage, adds or redraws material, rebuilds the audio and reorganizes the work. Toei's own choice of “enhanced edition” is more precise than forcing the project into one familiar category.

This matters because arguments about “which version is canon” often obscure the more interesting question: what experience is each version trying to deliver? The movie is comparatively compact. The original television arc belongs to the cadence of the longer series. The 2026 edition offers a revised encounter for viewers who want contemporary production treatment without losing the story's origins.

If someone has never seen Beerus before, the enhanced edition can function as an entry into this portion of the franchise. If someone knows the original film by heart, the appeal is noticing changes in presentation. Those are different audiences, and a useful guide should say so.

What is new for U.S. viewers?

Crunchyroll announced the enhanced series for streaming beginning October 11, 2026. A separate U.S. distributor release confirmed English, Spanish and Portuguese dubbed episodes beginning November 12 at 8 a.m. Pacific Time, with weekly installments. That second date is about the dubbed release, not the series' original Japanese debut; mixing the two would create an incorrect schedule.

Before paying for a different platform to chase a supposed exclusive, check the official Crunchyroll program page and audio options. Franchise catalogs can have separate entries for the 2013 film, the original television anime and the 2026 enhanced version. Their similar artwork and names make it easy to click the wrong one.

Does Beerus lead directly to new Dragon Ball material?

Toei also announced Dragon Ball Super: The Galactic Patrol, describing it as a new project that follows the Universe Survival arc and adapts the Galactic Patrol Prisoner material from the manga. That is a different production. The distinction should be visible whenever Beerus is discussed alongside the franchise's future: one project reinterprets an early story, while the other moves beyond the original television run.

A headline such as “Dragon Ball Super returns with a new story” would therefore be a poor way to describe the Beerus edition on its own. It would conflate the enhanced retelling with the separately announced continuation and promise the reader something they may not receive.

Which version should you watch first?

For someone with limited time who wants to understand why Beerus is important, Battle of Gods offers a self-contained introduction. For someone following the entire Dragon Ball Super television narrative, the original Beerus Saga still explains how the franchise moved into its later arcs. For an established viewer curious about the 2026 production choices, Dragon Ball Super: Beerus is the obvious place to start a comparison.

There is no obligation to watch all three. However, if you do compare them, look at a few consistent dimensions: how much attention each gives the initial threat, how Vegeta's anxiety is played for tension and comedy, how the ritual and the battle are paced, and how sound makes Beerus's power feel distinctive. Comparing these choices tells us more than treating every redrawn cut as a historical correction.

The reason this retelling matters

The Beerus story marks a shift in Dragon Ball's idea of power. In the older battles, a villain often stood at the top of a hierarchy and had to be defeated. Beerus unsettles that pattern: he can destroy, but he's also a character whose tastes, moods and curiosity shape the encounter. Goku cannot solve every difference in strength by immediately finding the next transformation. That creates space for humor, vulnerability and a universe much bigger than one planet.

The enhanced edition gives creators the chance to reconsider how that tonal shift looks and sounds for audiences watching in 2026. Whether any particular shot succeeds is a matter for scene-level criticism, not press-release repetition. But the confirmed changes are substantial enough to make Beerus more than a relabeled streaming listing.

Bottom line

Dragon Ball Super: Beerus is an enhanced reconstruction of the early Beerus storyline, not a newly announced sequel to the Tournament of Power. The production changes are officially documented, the U.S. streaming launch is confirmed, and separate dub availability began in November. Watch it for the reinterpretation; look to the separately announced Galactic Patrol project for the franchise's next major narrative step.
