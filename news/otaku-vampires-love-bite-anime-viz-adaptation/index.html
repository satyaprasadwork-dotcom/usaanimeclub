Otaku Vampire's Love Bite Gets a TV Anime: VIZ Reveals First Teaser

VIZ announced a television adaptation of Julietta Suzuki's Otaku Vampire's Love Bite on November 3, 2026, with teaser visuals but no premiere date.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

VIZ has announced a TV anime adaptation of Julietta Suzuki's Otaku Vampire's Love Bite and released a first teaser trailer and key art; casting and premiere details remain to be announced.

Source record: November 3, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What does the teaser confirm about the adaptation?

Distinguish the announced television project from any unannounced US streaming or English dub plans.

The announcement and its limits

On November 3, VIZ Media announced that Otaku Vampire's Love Bite, a manga from Kamisama Kiss creator Julietta Suzuki, is receiving a television anime adaptation. The publisher released a first teaser and key visual with the announcement. It has not yet confirmed a premiere date, full cast or production staff in that release.

VIZ's announcement establishes that an adaptation is being developed; it does not tell US readers which streaming service will show it or whether any English-language dub is planned. Those details are important but currently unanswered, and we will not fill them in with guesses.

What the manga is about

The original supernatural romantic comedy follows Hina, a vampire whose interest in anime fandom is stronger than her interest in drinking blood. When she moves to Japan for otaku activities, her neighbor Kyuta happens to resemble her favorite fictional character. His blood also attracts other vampires, setting up an unusual combination of romantic comedy and supernatural complications.

VIZ notes that the manga began in Hakusensha's Hana to Yume magazine in November 2022 and reached English-language publication through VIZ in October 2024. That makes the original manga an established route for English-reading fans who want the story while anime details develop.

What US fans should monitor

An adaptation teaser can confirm a project without settling distribution territory, release season, or staff. The next concrete news milestones are an official broadcaster or platform announcement, a dated broadcast window and confirmed creative credits. Even after a Japanese date appears, an American stream still requires separate geographic verification.

The relevant source is VIZ's own press release, which also identifies the intellectual-property rights holders. The original announcement links authorized footage and rights-holder credits without requiring unauthorized reuse of character images.

What the teaser confirms and what it deliberately leaves open

VIZ's November 3 press release is an adaptation announcement, not a full seasonal premiere package. It identifies Julietta Suzuki's original manga, establishes that a television anime is being made and links a first teaser trailer and key art. The release explicitly says production staff, casting and premiere details will be announced later. That final sentence matters more than it might seem: an entertainment headline should not convert a teaser into a confirmed streaming date or fill in cast names from fan wishes. A project can be officially greenlit long before viewers know when or where to watch it.

VIZ describes the teaser as drawing on imagery from the source manga. Readers should therefore be careful about treating every frame as representative of finished television animation. Preproduction promotional materials can establish the characters and premise without confirming how episodes will be directed or animated. The rights notice attached to the official announcement is also a reminder that these are copyrighted designs. For an independent news site, it is more responsible to link readers to the publisher's authorized materials than to upload unlicensed art and call it an original image.

American distribution is another unanswered question. VIZ's role as an English-language publisher and its participation in an announcement do not automatically make a particular US streaming service the anime's home. Even if a series eventually receives an American simulcast, its subtitling, dubbing and release windows may be decided later. A credible update would name a streaming partner, an exact season or both. Until that happens, the useful public statement is simply that the anime adaptation is in development, with its first teaser now available from VIZ.

The manga's appeal rests on a fandom joke with real character stakes

Hina is not presented as a conventional terrifying vampire. The publisher describes a recluse who has stayed inside for thirty years and whose deep attachment to a fictional anime character motivates her to pursue fandom activities in Japan. VIZ's series page explains that she had discovered the anime Vampire Cross and become absorbed in the character Mao. That premise provides a comedic mismatch between a supernatural creature's traditional appetite and the ordinary fan impulse to travel, collect merchandise and meet people who share an obsession.

The arrival of her neighbor Kyuta complicates the joke. He resembles Hina's favorite fictional character, and his blood draws attention from other vampires. This turns a private fan fixation into a relationship with another person who has his own identity and circumstances. The setup has room for romantic comedy and supernatural conflict, but it also raises a character question: can Hina separate a real neighbor from the idealized figure she admires? That is an interpretation of the official premise, not a claim about how the manga or eventual anime resolves the relationship.

The project's lineage is relevant but should not be overstated. Julietta Suzuki is known to English-speaking readers for Kamisama Kiss, and VIZ cites that work in its headline because it helps readers place the creator. It would be inaccurate, however, to call Otaku Vampire's Love Bite a sequel or assume the same characters, studio, cast or romance structure will recur. It is a separate manga title. VIZ says the series was serialized in Hakusensha's Hana to Yume from November 2022 and reached English publication through VIZ in October 2024, establishing a legitimate way to explore the original work now.

How to follow the project without getting misled

Readers can start with the authorized manga edition and the publisher's teaser rather than copying entertainment-site rumor timelines. The VIZ title page includes a synopsis and links to its English volumes. Those are relevant because they provide an existing, legally published source while an anime date is unknown. Do not equate the English manga's release schedule with a future television episode timetable. The two media have different contracts, production requirements and territorial arrangements, and nothing in the November press release establishes a simultaneous publication strategy.

Fans tracking adaptations can use a simple three-stage checklist. First, note the announcement itself and the underlying manga. Second, watch for named production staff and actors from the project or a rights holder. Third, record the Japanese broadcast date and the US streaming rights independently. If a news account announces an English dub with no source, the claim should be treated as unverified even if it sounds plausible. This method is especially useful for projects with a strong online fanbase, because repeated guesses can look like consensus despite having no original evidence.

For now, the meaningful development is that a supernatural romance manga with an established English edition is moving toward television animation. The November press release does not identify a US streaming service, provide advance episodes or include interviews with the production team. When genuine production details appear, a follow-up should report exactly what changed and link the source, leaving the November article as the record of the initial announcement. That is more informative than repeatedly refreshing a headline with unverified launch windows.
