Itachi and Shisui Explained: Kotoamatsukami, the Crow and the Failed Plan

Why was Shisui so important to Itachi? Follow the two eyes, Danzō's intervention, Kotoamatsukami and Naruto's crow through the war—without fan myths.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

Shisui was Itachi's trusted friend and mentor. Hoping to stop the Uchiha coup without slaughter, he planned to use Kotoamatsukami, but Danzō seized his right eye. Shisui entrusted his remaining eye to Itachi, who later concealed it in a crow linked to Naruto. During the war the technique unexpectedly freed reanimated Itachi from Kabuto's control. The original peace plan failed; its safeguard later succeeded in another context.

Two friends who were trying to solve the same problem

Itachi and Shisui are often remembered as a pair of exceptionally powerful Uchiha with tragic stories. That shorthand misses the reason their relationship matters. They were trying to prevent a political confrontation from becoming a massacre. Shisui imagined a possible intervention that would stop the coup without killing the clan, while Itachi feared the violence gathering on both sides. Their friendship developed from training and mutual trust into shared responsibility for a crisis that grown leaders failed to resolve.

The official Naruto Shisui birthday column gives us an unusually clear evidence trail. It names their friendship, explains the ability of Kotoamatsukami, records the seizure of one eye, and follows the other eye all the way to Itachi's unexpected liberation during the war. This article traces that chain without assuming every supplementary anime scene is identical to the manga. The story is spoiler-heavy for the Uchiha massacre and the Fourth Great Ninja War.

Who was Shisui to Itachi?

Shisui was more than another talented clan member. He was a close friend whom Itachi looked up to as an older brother figure. Their relationship offered something Itachi rarely found elsewhere: a person with whom he could discuss the demands of loyalty without pretending that the village and clan were always right. They trained together, questioned what it meant to be a shinobi and saw the growing danger in the Uchiha political conflict. A younger Itachi found a kind of moral companion before the choices around him became catastrophic.

That history is easy to overlook if you enter Naruto through brief clips of Shisui's eyes. His importance is not reducible to the genjutsu he possessed. He represented the possibility that power could be used to de-escalate rather than dominate. The anime's Itachi Shinden episodes expand their time together, while the official franchise column points back to manga chapters showing the friendship and its consequences. Both are useful, but they tell the story through different emphases.

What is Kotoamatsukami?

Kotoamatsukami is Shisui's exceptionally subtle genjutsu. Unlike an illusion that obviously changes the scenery or traps someone in a visible nightmare, it can influence thoughts and intentions so that the target experiences the resulting decision as their own. The official Naruto profile describes the manipulation as capable of passing unnoticed. That makes it terrifying in a political crisis: a person's apparent agreement might be genuine persuasion—or a choice secretly planted through an ocular technique.

The ability also introduces a moral puzzle. Shisui's purpose was to prevent bloodshed. But changing a leader's mind without that leader knowing it has happened is not ordinary negotiation. Even when the desired outcome is peace, the method erases the target's independent consent. Naruto uses extraordinary powers to make familiar ethical problems difficult to ignore. If stopping a violent coup requires making someone believe a thought that was never theirs, how different is that from the coercion the heroes say they oppose?

Why did Danzō take Shisui's eye?

Danzō did not trust the proposed intervention. According to the official retrospective, he seized Shisui's right eye during the effort to prevent the coup. That removed an important tool Shisui hoped to use and left the young shinobi with narrowing alternatives. It also showed how people charged with protecting the village could treat the bodies and abilities of their own allies as resources to seize. The episode is not just one villain acquiring a powerful item; it is a political move that damages any chance of solving the crisis without force.

Fans sometimes describe this incident as a clean debate between Shisui's flawless peace plan and Danzō's inevitable massacre. It was neither. Kotoamatsukami's own coercive nature creates uncomfortable questions, and the story does not give us a demonstrated alternate timeline in which its use solves everything permanently. What is shown is that Danzō's action undermines an attempted alternative and deepens the tragedy. Itachi is left carrying a trust that the institution has already betrayed.

Why did Shisui give Itachi his remaining eye?

After losing the right eye, Shisui entrusts his remaining eye to Itachi and places his hopes for the village and Uchiha future with him. His death is the breaking point in Itachi's life, closely tied to the awakening of Itachi's Mangekyō Sharingan. It is important not to turn the event into a convenient power-up story. A friend dies, a possible peaceful intervention disappears and a young person is pushed further toward a decision he finds intolerable.

Shisui's gift also must be tracked correctly. The right eye taken by Danzō and the left eye given to Itachi are not the same physical object moving through one uninterrupted chain of owners. Many simplified explainers collapse them into a single magical eye that 'went from Danzō to Naruto.' That is wrong. The official column makes the two routes clear. Keeping them separate answers much of the apparent mystery that follows.

Why did Itachi put a crow inside Naruto?

Years later Itachi uses a crow as a way of carrying Shisui's surviving eye and its Kotoamatsukami ability. He places this safeguard with Naruto, who is determined to bring Sasuke back from his destructive path. Itachi expects that Sasuke may eventually become dangerous to Konoha and wants a contingency that might redirect him without simply killing him. The choice is deeply revealing. Even from outside the village, Itachi is trying to manage Sasuke's future through another person's intervention.

One should resist the temptation to describe the crow as a constant all-purpose guardian that can independently solve whatever problem arises. It is a highly specific arrangement with a particular trigger and an important eye technique. Naruto does not receive a permanent inventory of Itachi's powers. Instead, he unknowingly carries a safeguard whose eventual activation takes place in conditions Itachi did not originally intend. That difference is what makes its later appearance dramatic.

What happened when the crow appeared during the war?

After Kabuto reanimates Itachi, Naruto and Killer Bee encounter the revived Uchiha and Nagato. The crow emerges from Naruto and Kotoamatsukami acts on Itachi rather than Sasuke. The effect overrides the control exerted by the Reanimation Jutsu, leaving Itachi free to act on his own. The official Naruto anniversary account explicitly traces this sequence. It is an astonishing reversal: a technique meant to restrain a possible future version of Sasuke instead frees the man who arranged it.

This is not the same as Kotoamatsukami instantly defeating Kabuto. The technique releases Itachi from one form of control; Itachi must still work out how to confront the source of the reanimation and end the jutsu. He goes on to cooperate with Sasuke against Kabuto. The eye has fulfilled a protective purpose, but not the one assigned to it in the original plan. A strategy that depended on controlling Sasuke becomes, unexpectedly, a way for Itachi to correct some of his own mistakes.

A simple eye-by-eye record that avoids the common myths

Start with the right eye: Danzō seizes it from Shisui. Its later significance is tied to the schemes and power of Danzō himself. Then follow the left eye: Shisui entrusts it to Itachi; Itachi preserves its power in a crow associated with Naruto; the crow activates when reanimated Itachi meets Naruto and frees him from Kabuto's command. Those are two separate routes. Keeping the record this simple is more useful than any complicated drawing filled with speculative transfers.

Next separate native abilities from borrowed ones. Itachi's Tsukuyomi and Amaterasu are associated with his own Mangekyō powers. Kotoamatsukami is Shisui's distinctive technique, available later through the preserved eye. Finally separate intention from outcome: the eye's first purpose was a contingency for Sasuke, while its actual decisive use concerns Itachi. These three distinctions clear up the most persistent confusion around the crow without inventing extra rules.

Was Shisui's plan morally better than Itachi's?

Shisui sought an alternative to killing the clan; in that obvious sense, his stated objective was less catastrophic. But a plan to influence a person's mind without awareness is still ethically troubling. Itachi's eventual massacre is worse in its scale and irreversible harm. The story's richness comes from refusing to make secret power the easy solution to a crisis produced by ordinary discrimination, fear and political control.

Both young shinobi were trying to repair a system from the inside while leaders with more authority acted to preserve their own position. Their abilities were remarkable, but neither possessed the institutional power to guarantee an honest settlement. Reading the events as a contest between the best genjutsu and the strongest political faction misses that difference. The deepest tragedy is how readily people entrusted a problem of governance to children carrying weapons.

Quick answers about Shisui and the crow

Did Shisui die before the Uchiha massacre? Yes, his death occurs during the rising crisis before the fateful night. Did Danzō take both eyes? No; Shisui's remaining eye was entrusted to Itachi. Is Kotoamatsukami Itachi's natural Mangekyō technique? No, the technique belongs to Shisui and is used later through Shisui's preserved eye. Why did it activate on Itachi? The contingency was triggered during the reanimated encounter with Naruto, producing an unintended but decisive result.

Did the crow make Itachi permanently alive? No. He remained a reanimated shinobi who later departed when the technique was undone. Could the original peace plan definitely have prevented the massacre? The story never shows that alternate future. The dependable reference points are the official Shisui birthday column and the official Great Ninja War retrospective, which together record the two eye routes and the later activation.
