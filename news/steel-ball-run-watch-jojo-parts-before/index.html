JoJo Watch Order: Parts 1–7 and Where Steel Ball Run Fits

A spoiler-light JoJo watch order for newcomers: Phantom Blood through Stone Ocean, why release order works, and why Steel Ball Run can stand alone.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 18, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 at 2:24 PM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

For a first watch, follow the anime release order: Phantom Blood, Battle Tendency, Stardust Crusaders, Diamond Is Unbreakable, Golden Wind, Stone Ocean, then Steel Ball Run. Steel Ball Run is Part 7 in a new continuity and can be understood on its own, although earlier parts add franchise context. Netflix’s 2026 release schedule is separate from the recommended story order.

Source record: September 25, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Where does Steel Ball Run fit in the JoJo watch order?

The first-watch release order is Parts 1–6, then Steel Ball Run. Part 7 begins a new continuity and can be watched independently; prior parts add franchise context, not required plot setup.

The quick answer: do you need Parts 1–6 first?

No. Steel Ball Run is JoJo's Bizarre Adventure Part 7, but it opens a new continuity and a new cast. You can follow Johnny Joestar, Gyro Zeppeli, and the cross-country race without finishing the six earlier anime parts. If Netflix has brought you to JoJo through the 2026 adaptation, there is no prerequisite episode list you must complete before pressing play.

For a first journey through the franchise, though, the simplest order is still the anime release order: Phantom Blood, Battle Tendency, Stardust Crusaders, Diamond Is Unbreakable, Golden Wind, Stone Ocean, and then Steel Ball Run. That sequence lets the original continuity's family legacy and the series' evolving style unfold naturally. The difference matters: “not required” does not mean “irrelevant,” and “Part 7” does not mean it is Season 7 of one uninterrupted plot.

The recommended anime order at a glance

The table gives the practical first-watch route. Use the part names as your navigation because streaming platforms may split the same story into seasons, cours, or batches with different labels. The franchise's numbering refers to manga story parts. It does not always match the number of rows in an app's season selector, and a listing for an OVA or special may be filed elsewhere.

Parts 1 through 6 share a broad continuity, but every part shifts its lead, setting, and often its central supernatural rules. Starting from the beginning helps explain recurring names and the idea of the Joestar inheritance. It also shows why JoJo's tone changes over time: an early gothic adventure becomes a globe-spanning Stand story, then a small-town mystery, a gang drama, and finally a prison escape narrative.

Why Part 7 can be a fresh starting point

Steel Ball Run is set in a different version of the world and begins a new sequence of events. Its protagonists and immediate stakes are introduced within the race itself. Viewers do not need the plot recap of Parts 1–6 to understand why Johnny wants to walk again, why Gyro is riding across the continent, or what the race means to the people competing in it. The show teaches the rules it needs as those characters encounter them.

There are still rewarding echoes for longtime fans: names, visual ideas, character archetypes, and franchise themes reappear in transformed ways. Those are references and reinterpretations, not missing plot chapters. A newcomer can notice them as flavor without decoding every callback. If you enjoy Steel Ball Run and want more afterward, going back to Part 1 makes the franchise's older history feel like a bonus path rather than required homework.

What the original continuity adds

Watching Parts 1–6 first gives a richer understanding of the Joestar name and of the way each generation inherits unfinished conflicts. Phantom Blood establishes the foundational rivalry; Battle Tendency expands the family story; Stardust Crusaders gives the modern series its iconic Stand battles. Diamond Is Unbreakable, Golden Wind, and Stone Ocean then use that foundation to tell stories that can each be followed through a new local cast and goal.

This is a good route for viewers who care about the franchise as a whole or want to recognize its visual vocabulary. It is also the best route for someone who dislikes pausing mid-series to ask whether a callback is important. The tradeoff is time: there are six animated parts before Steel Ball Run, with changing pacing and a first part that feels much older in style. You can sample the first episodes and decide whether that history is part of the fun for you.

Release order versus chronology

Release order is the easiest first-watch order because it follows how the anime introduces its concepts. A strict chronology chart is less helpful: JoJo's parts jump between generations, eras, and continuities, while some special episodes adapt side stories or material that fits better after a particular arc. Chronological rearrangement can make a viewer bounce between casts without improving the central plot.

For a first viewing, stay with numbered parts in release order. Once you know the main story, you can explore OVAs, recap films, or supplementary episodes if you want a different cut. Do not treat a recap film as a required substitute for a season, and check whether a title labeled “JoJo's Bizarre Adventure” is the TV adaptation, a compilation, or an unrelated special before adding it to a watchlist.

Netflix’s 2026 Steel Ball Run release is a separate question

Netflix Tudum describes the first Steel Ball Run stage as a 47-minute premiere episode. Its subsequent schedule groups the remaining stages into 11 episodes released weekly, beginning with the second stage on September 25, 2026. This is the platform's rollout information, not a change to JoJo's story order. Viewers should use the current Netflix title page for the actual US episode listing and any change to the weekly calendar.

The difference between a premiere stage and a standard weekly episode explains why early coverage may call the launch a special or a first stage. Do not infer a full-season episode total from the first runtime alone. For current dub availability, check the Netflix audio menu for the US account; a language track is a catalog fact that can vary by region and may be added on a different schedule from subtitles.

A low-spoiler route for different kinds of viewers

If you want the whole experience, begin with Phantom Blood and continue through the numbered parts. If you want a quick test, watch a few episodes of Phantom Blood and then decide whether the classic origin works for you. If Netflix brought you specifically for Steel Ball Run, start with Part 7 and come back to the earlier continuity later. None of these choices damages the story; they simply prioritize different kinds of context.

If you are watching with a friend, agree on the goal before choosing: franchise history, the fastest route to SBR, or a taste test for the series' tone. That prevents an argument about whether six parts are “mandatory” when the actual answer depends on why you are watching. The best route is the one that makes you want to keep going while preserving the distinction between story continuity and franchise references.

The practical recommendation

Start with Phantom Blood if you want JoJo's generational story in the order it was written for anime viewers. Start with Steel Ball Run if the Netflix adaptation is what interests you and you want an independent entry. In either case, use official platform listings to confirm which seasons are currently in the US catalog; the franchise watch order does not promise that one service carries every part at once.

Steel Ball Run is a clean doorway, not a gate that only opens after six seasons. The older parts make the larger franchise more rewarding, while Part 7 stands on its own well enough to welcome new viewers. That balance is the useful answer to the question: watch the earlier parts when you want the history, not because the race is impossible to understand without them.
