Itachi Watch Order: Essential Naruto Episodes, Shippuden Arc and Novels

Where to start Itachi's story without spoiling Naruto: a spoiler-safe viewing route, Shippuden episodes 451–458, official numbering differences and VIZ novels.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

First-time viewers should follow Naruto and Naruto Shippuden in original story order, meeting Itachi as Sasuke does and saving the Itachi Shinden flashbacks for later. For a character-focused rewatch, the Itachi Shinden arc runs through Naruto Shippuden episodes 451–458 on common US listings; Naruto's official all-franchise episode index numbers the same arc 671–678. VIZ also publishes Itachi's Story: Daylight and Midnight novels, which are supplementary, not interchangeable with the manga.

Watching Naruto for the first time or revisiting Itachi's full story?

First-time: follow the main story. Rewatch: follow childhood, massacre, Akatsuki, Sasuke duel and war. Itachi Shinden is Shippuden 451–458, or 671–678 in the combined official episode count.

There are two good watch orders, and they serve different viewers

Searching for the Itachi episodes can be surprisingly frustrating. One guide recommends a childhood flashback first; another starts with the famous brother-versus-brother battle; a third lists an episode number that appears far beyond the end of some US streaming seasons. None of these is necessarily malicious. The confusion comes from different goals and numbering systems, as well as the fact that Itachi's motives were deliberately hidden when the audience first met him.

Choose the route by your situation. If you are watching Naruto for the first time, follow the main story order. Itachi works as a mystery because Sasuke's version of him comes first. If you already know the major twist, a focused Itachi rewatch can move chronologically through childhood, the massacre, Akatsuki, the duel and the war. If you prefer reading, the original Naruto manga and the separately published Itachi novels give you another route. This guide separates those choices instead of claiming one list is perfect for everyone.

First-time viewers: preserve the mystery

The least frustrating spoiler-safe route is the ordinary progression from Naruto into Naruto Shippuden. That lets you meet Itachi through Kakashi's frightening encounter, Sasuke's reaction and the developing mystery of the Akatsuki. His apparent cruelty is part of how Naruto tells the story. Watching the backstory adaptation first would explain hidden motives before the characters have earned the truth, changing the impact of scenes that depend on uncertainty.

At this stage it is better to resist compilations titled The Full Truth About Itachi. They often contain unmarked footage of the massacre, his final fight and his war-arc farewell. Those scenes may be beautiful, but they reveal key story turns years before a first-time viewer reaches them. If you want a small preview, use official introductory character material rather than an algorithm-driven edit that jumps between decades of story chronology. The point is to encounter the mystery in the order it was written.

For returning fans: a five-stage Itachi story route

An effective rewatch divides the material into five stages. First comes childhood and the political crisis, where Itachi's ideals form and his relationship with Shisui becomes central. Second is the massacre and departure into the Akatsuki. Third comes his role as a threat in the original Naruto era and as a distant figure influencing Sasuke's choices. Fourth is the final brothers' fight and the revelation of its hidden context. Fifth is his reanimated return, cooperation with Sasuke and last farewell during the Fourth Great Ninja War.

This is a story path rather than a promise that each phase fits one season block. The episodes are interleaved with Naruto's much wider ensemble plot. In a rewatch, that is an advantage: you can follow Itachi's personal arc while remembering that the world did not stop moving between his appearances. But if you are new, use this progression only after you are comfortable knowing why he joined Akatsuki and what happened to the Uchiha.

Where does the Itachi Shinden arc begin?

On common Naruto Shippuden episode listings in the United States, the Itachi Shinden: Light and Darkness flashback runs across episodes 451–458. The installments include Birth and Death, The Genius, The Pain of Living, Shisui's Request, Moonlit Night, The Darkness of the Akatsuki, Partner and Truth. Licensed Google Play listings present this sequence under Shippuden episode numbers. These names are useful when a service splits the show into unusual season packs or omits the numbering entirely.

The official Naruto franchise website uses a different numbering system on its English anime archive. It lists the same Itachi's Story: Daylight / Midnight arc as episodes 671–678 because that index numbers Naruto and Shippuden episodes across a combined sequence. It does not mean there are two different eight-episode Itachi arcs. You can verify that the opening Birth and Death installment appears as official index episode 671 while a US listing calls it Shippuden episode 451. Both point to the same story.

What is actually in those eight episodes?

The arc follows Itachi as a gifted child confronted by the cost of war, then shows his training and his attempts to navigate clan and village politics. Shisui is especially important because the story gives the pair time together before the crisis destroys their shared plan. The later episodes connect Itachi's ANBU role, Danzō's influence, the Uchiha coup and the night of the massacre. Afterward, the series turns toward his relationships within Akatsuki and the fractured connection to Sasuke.

The sequence is useful because it lets the audience live with the consequences in ordinary scenes rather than learn everything from one later explanation. Still, the official anime index labels this arc anime-original. The episodes draw from a larger body of franchise material but are not a word-for-word reproduction of the original manga or both novels. If you want to discuss specific dialogue or details, identify whether you mean the manga, the novels or the television adaptation; the distinctions matter.

When should you watch the brothers' decisive fight?

The emotional center of Itachi's story is his confrontation with Sasuke late in the latter's pursuit of revenge. It belongs in the main Shippuden progression, after Sasuke has spent years becoming the person Itachi wanted him to be. The battle itself includes genjutsu exchanges, increasingly dangerous attacks and an ending whose meaning changes dramatically when the next stage of the story reveals Itachi's background.

Rather than give a single season number—which differs sharply by service—follow the official Naruto Master's Prophecy and Vengeance arc and the manga's corresponding material in volumes 42–44. The Naruto official retrospective identifies the brothers' confrontation and the relevant chapters. This route protects readers from landing on the wrong episode because a streaming platform calls one part Season 8 while another service calls the same segment something different. Look for the arc and title, then verify the episode on your platform.

Do not skip the war-arc farewell

The original duel does not conclude everything Itachi has to say. His reanimated return during the war brings him into contact with Naruto and Killer Bee, then with Sasuke again. The brothers cooperate against Kabuto to undo the Reanimation Jutsu, giving them a brief chance to act as allies instead of enemies. This second encounter is essential to understanding how Itachi finally addresses the truth he kept hidden and how Sasuke makes his next decision.

The official Naruto episode index includes I Will Love You Always as episode 559 in the all-franchise sequence. The matching main-story placement can be located through the Great Ninja War: Sasuke and Itachi arc; pay attention to the arc title if your streaming provider renumbers it. This is why a viewing list that stops at the original death is incomplete. Itachi's final moments in the war do not magically undo his earlier actions, but they mark a shift from trying to steer Sasuke to allowing him to choose.

Reading Naruto: manga first or supplemental novels?

Masashi Kishimoto's Naruto manga is the cleanest foundation for the main canon narrative. The official Naruto birthday retrospect cites chapter and volume locations for key Itachi scenes, including the Uchiha political revelation in volume 43 and the later final farewell in volume 62. Those references are far more helpful than random image boards of isolated panels. VIZ provides an authorized English catalog for readers in the United States, with chapter and volume access governed by its own offerings.

VIZ also publishes Naruto: Itachi's Story: Daylight and Midnight. Its Daylight introduction describes Itachi's early experience of war and the layers of loyalty that make him difficult to judge. The novels add an extended perspective that differs in presentation from the main manga; the anime's adaptation also makes its own choices. We would not advise treating every anime-only scene as an interchangeable statement of manga fact. For a reader who wants the deepest view, read the manga's central reveal first, then use the two novels for context and comparison.

Spoiler levels: which route is safe for you?

There are three useful levels. Level one: you have just met Itachi and know only that Sasuke hates him. Stay with the main series. Level two: you have finished the brothers' fight and learned the political truth. You can watch the Itachi Shinden flashback without losing the big reveal, though it may still introduce details from much later in Shippuden. Level three: you have reached the Fourth Great Ninja War. Now the entire character route—including Shisui's crow, Izanami and the final farewell—can be revisited without accidentally reading ahead.

These levels are an editorial convenience, not official content labels. They help readers decide what sort of knowledge they want before clicking. This is better than classifying a whole eight-episode arc as harmless filler and telling new fans to skip it or watch it first. Filler labels describe adaptation origin, not whether something carries emotional value. Itachi's flashbacks can be worthwhile precisely because they spend time on experiences the main story compresses.

The US viewing question: why can't one platform link solve this?

Streaming services license anime by region, package episodes differently and sometimes divide English dubbing into separate availability windows. A screenshot of one catalog is therefore not proof that every US subscriber can watch an episode in that arrangement. Search the official title page for Naruto Shippuden and confirm the series, audio choice and installment title. If the visible listing says 671 while your app ends Shippuden at 500, the difference may be the combined official franchise counter rather than a missing episode.

Do not confuse a retail availability listing with a blanket statement about subscription streaming. Google Play, VIZ and other authorized services have distinct models, and the legal availability of one episode can change. Our numbered guide describes where the story appears, not a guarantee that a particular package is free or included today. It is worth taking a minute to check rather than purchasing a season that begins after the scene you actually wanted.

Quick Itachi episode and reading questions

Are Shippuden episodes 451–458 worth watching? For fans who want the character's upbringing, Shisui relationship and political background, yes; but first-time viewers may prefer to save them for after the central reveal. Why does the official Naruto site say 671–678? Its index combines Naruto and Shippuden numbering, while US platforms commonly number Shippuden alone. Do the VIZ novels equal these episodes? No. They are related licensed prose works, not duplicate files of the broadcast scripts.

Can I watch only the Itachi scenes? You can, but Sasuke's long emotional development and the politics of Konoha will be harder to understand. Does the story end when Itachi first dies? No; the war arc returns him through reanimation for a second farewell. What is the spoiler-safe starting point? Begin with the original Naruto series and continue in its release-story sequence. That is still the most rewarding way to discover who Itachi was before reading an explanation.
