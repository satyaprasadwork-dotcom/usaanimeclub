Who Is the Strongest Curse in Jujutsu Kaisen? Sukuna, Rika, Mahito and Jogo Explained

Is Sukuna the strongest curse in JJK, or does the answer change if you mean cursed spirits? A spoiler-aware guide to Rika, Mahito, Jogo, Dagon, Hanami and cursed power.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

Sukuna is the 'King of Curses' and one of Jujutsu Kaisen's most formidable beings, but he is not an ordinary cursed spirit. Among actual cursed spirits, the story offers strong candidates such as the original special-grade vengeful spirit Rika, Mahito and Jogo. No single official all-series ranking resolves every matchup, so the answer depends on what 'curse' means and which version of each character you are comparing.

Source record: November 15, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Are you comparing the King of Curses or actual cursed spirits?

Treat Sukuna separately as an ancient incarnated sorcerer. For actual cursed spirits, compare the original Rika, Mahito and Jogo by story version and matchup rather than quoting an invented official ranking.

The answer depends on one word: curse

The most common answer to 'who is the strongest curse in JJK?' is Ryomen Sukuna. There is a good reason: characters call him the King of Curses, his power shapes the entire plot, and the series treats his presence as a catastrophe. But there is a technical problem with answering only 'Sukuna.' He is not classified as an ordinary cursed spirit like Jogo or Mahito. If the question is specifically about a being created from cursed energy and negative human feelings, the comparison changes immediately.

This guide separates the broad fan shorthand from the narrower in-universe category. It discusses abilities shown in Jujutsu Kaisen 0, the first television season and the Shibuya Incident, then flags later-material context rather than hiding major manga developments in a supposedly spoiler-free answer. No official character roster supplies a universal first-to-last list of every spirit. What follows is a reasoned comparison, not an official power chart.

What is a cursed spirit, and why is Sukuna different?

Jujutsu Kaisen's premise is built around curses arising from negative human feelings. Ordinary people leak cursed energy, which can accumulate into spirits that threaten people and places. Jujutsu sorcerers learn to perceive and fight those beings. A cursed object is different: it can carry an imprint or dangerous power without itself being an independently roaming spirit. A human sorcerer and a reincarnated ancient sorcerer belong to different categories again. The shared vocabulary can be confusing when subtitles, fan discussions and character epithets all use the word curse.

Sukuna lived in the distant past and persists in the story through his divided remains as cursed objects, becoming incarnated through a human host. The official anime profile describes him as a being who lived more than a thousand years ago, whose remains became cursed objects, and whose fingers entered Yuji Itadori's body. Calling him the King of Curses is accurate; treating that title as proof he has the same nature as the disaster spirits is not. This distinction is the first fact any serious ranking has to get right.

Why Sukuna still leads the broad 'curse' conversation

With that qualification in place, Sukuna remains the obvious choice when fans mean the most terrifying curse-related fighter. He combines extraordinary destructive technique, tactical intelligence, a willingness to exploit weaknesses and the ability to overwhelm opponents who are themselves considered exceptional. He is not simply dangerous because he has a larger reserve of cursed energy. He is dangerous because he learns what another fighter is attempting and makes the consequences brutal. His reputation is earned inside the narrative, not just repeated by its characters.

Yet power-scaling shorthand often strips away the conditions that make fights intelligible. Sukuna's strength changes depending on how many fingers he has recovered, what body he is inhabiting and what resources are available during a particular conflict. Comparing a partial incarnation from an early episode with an enemy at their full strength is not the same as comparing their best demonstrated forms. For this reason, a precise matchup should name the story arc and version rather than declare that every appearance is identical.

Rika Orimoto: the strongest case among the original vengeful spirits

Jujutsu Kaisen 0 introduces Rika Orimoto as a special-grade vengeful cursed spirit tied to Yuta Okkotsu. Her importance is not just the amount of force she can exert. The film makes the tragedy of her attachment central to the story, and her extraordinary presence changes what other sorcerers think Yuta can do. As an example of a singular, exceptional curse that defies ordinary expectations, the original Rika is one of the strongest candidates a reader can raise in a cursed-spirit discussion.

There is a crucial timeline warning. Yuta's bond with the original Rika changes at the end of Jujutsu Kaisen 0, and later references to 'Rika' should not automatically be treated as the exact same spirit in the exact same state. The official anime character description says Yuta successfully released his childhood friend Rika's curse after the confrontation with Geto. A clean ranking should distinguish the special-grade vengeful spirit of the prequel from later manifestations or fighting arrangements. Otherwise the debate mixes two different story conditions.

Mahito: perhaps the most frightening ability in close combat

Mahito is born from human fear and hatred of other humans, and his technique operates on the soul. That means a seemingly small touch can be a much larger threat than an ordinary punch. He experiments on people with disturbing curiosity and learns rapidly in combat. His greatest advantage is not a straightforward 'attack stat'; it is that many opponents have no safe answer to a technique that changes the relationship between body and soul. His evolution during the anime makes him more dangerous than his early appearances suggest.

Mahito is also a good example of why a one-number ranking can fail. A combatant able to damage his soul or prevent his technique from taking effect may survive an encounter that would destroy another, physically stronger opponent. Yuji's unusual connection to Sukuna is relevant to their interactions, and Mahito's domain changes the threat still further. The question is not simply whether his explosions are larger than Jogo's. It is whether his opponent can endure or counter an ability designed to bypass ordinary durability.

Jogo: devastating range and speed with terrible matchups

Jogo represents the disaster side of the curse ecosystem, with fire and volcanic destruction that make him a serious threat across a wide battlefield. He can pressure multiple targets, shape the environment, and deploy a domain. The mistake is to treat his losses against exceptionally powerful opponents as evidence that he is weak. When a character repeatedly runs into the setting's highest tiers, the final score can conceal just how dangerous that character would be to everyone else.

A comparison between Jogo and Mahito depends on distance, timing, domain use and how quickly each recognizes the other's options. Jogo may have a devastating advantage in immediate area damage; Mahito may have the more punishing technique if he establishes contact. The story does not provide a simple numerical table settling their hypothetical full-power encounter in every setting. Readers can choose a favorite, but the argument becomes stronger when it names the exact condition that would decide the fight.

Hanami, Dagon and the difference between endurance and danger

Hanami's powers emphasize vegetation, pressure, environmental manipulation and resilience. Dagon's abilities develop into a dangerous domain-based threat, showing that a cursed spirit's danger cannot always be inferred from how calm or slow it initially appears. Both illustrate an important lesson from the series: cursed spirits are not clones with different elemental paint. Their techniques alter the battlefield itself, creating problems that raw strength alone may not solve.

Hanami can absorb punishment and force opponents to fight on uncomfortable terms. Dagon's domain creates an especially demanding situation for fighters who lack their own effective domain response. They are credible high-threat spirits even if a popularity list rarely places them above Mahito or Jogo. For a newcomer, these matchups also explain why sorcerer teamwork matters. Some opponents are not defeated by one perfectly timed finishing move; they are defeated by several characters discovering which part of the enemy's technique can be interrupted.

What about Choso, Kurourushi and later cursed beings?

Choso should not be casually included in an ordinary cursed-spirit list. The official anime identifies him as the incarnation of the first Death Painting Womb, a special-grade cursed object; his history and human relationships make him a different case from a disaster curse born directly out of mass fear. He has cursed energy and extraordinary techniques, but that does not erase his category. This is precisely the sort of mistake that spreads when a ranking copies character names without reading their backgrounds.

Kurourushi is another meaningful comparison once readers reach the Culling Game. The official character catalog identifies this cockroach-associated entity as a special-grade cursed spirit. Its existence broadens the conversation beyond the handful of early-series disaster spirits. However, discussing detailed later fights would spoil portions of the anime or manga for readers still catching up. The safe conclusion here is simply that a comprehensive ranking must specify where its spoiler boundary falls and what material it includes.

How to judge strength without pretending a fan list is canon

A useful comparison asks at least six questions. What version of the character is being measured? What has the character actually done on-page or on-screen? Is the technique effective against many kinds of opponents or especially lethal against a few? Can the character use a domain, and what happens when domains collide? How well does the character recover from damage? Finally, did a loss come against an ordinary peer or one of the franchise's uniquely overwhelming fighters?

Those questions still do not yield perfect numbers. A dangerous soul technique may outperform greater destructive force in one matchup and fail completely in another. A spirit with a domain may dominate an opponent who has no response but struggle against one who can neutralize the sure-hit effect. If we must choose, the original Rika has a compelling claim as an exceptional singular vengeful spirit, Mahito offers a frightening lethal ability, and Jogo is extraordinarily destructive. None of those observations creates an official consensus champion among all spirits.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sukuna a curse or a human? He is called the King of Curses, but the story presents him as an ancient sorcerer who persists through cursed objects and incarnation rather than an ordinary spirit born from negative human emotion. Is Gojo a curse? No, he is a human jujutsu sorcerer. Is Rika stronger than Jogo? There is no official all-conditions verdict; it depends on which Rika and what matchup is being imagined.

Is Mahito the strongest cursed spirit? His soul-manipulating technique makes him a contender for the most dangerous, but 'most dangerous to many people' and 'wins every duel' are different claims. Does special grade mean two characters are equally powerful? No. It identifies exceptional threats but is not a shared numerical score. Should manga-only forms count? They can in a spoiler-labeled manga analysis, but they should not be slipped into an article promising an anime-accessible explanation.
