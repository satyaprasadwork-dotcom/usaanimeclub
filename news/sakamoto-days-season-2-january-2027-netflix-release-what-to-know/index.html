Sakamoto Days Season 2 Sets January 18, 2027 Premiere as New Trailer Reveals Creepy Nuts Opening

Sakamoto Days Season 2 now has a January 18, 2027 Japanese premiere, a new trailer and a Creepy Nuts opening. Here is what Netflix viewers in the US should know.

USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 at 9:28 AM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

Source record: October 15, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What is new in the November 18 Sakamoto Days update?

The exact January 18, 2027 Japanese debut and Creepy Nuts opening theme are new; check Netflix separately for a local episode time.

Overview

January is suddenly a more concrete date for Sakamoto Days fans. After months of knowing that a second season was coming in early 2027, viewers now have a specific Japanese premiere: January 18, 2027. A new promotional video also brings a music announcement that fits this series remarkably well. Creepy Nuts will perform the opening theme, titled “Papa wa Imada ni Juvenile.”

For anyone in the United States who has been seeing different release dates in old search results, there is an important distinction. Netflix's earlier material confirmed a January 2027 return; the newer Japanese announcement narrows that to January 18. Netflix is the established international home of the adaptation, but a local listing is still the best place to check the exact hour an episode becomes playable in your time zone. A Japanese television start date should not automatically be turned into a precise U.S. release time.

For readers comparing search snippets, a January 2027 streaming window and the Japanese January 18 broadcast date are not contradictory: one is a month-long announcement and the other is a more exact premiere detail. Neither source alone is a guarantee that every United States Netflix profile will display the next episode at the same clock time. If you're planning a viewing party, save the official Netflix series page, confirm its local availability close to release, and leave enough flexibility for different subtitle or audio options. This also helps avoid spoilers from speculation about the latest promotional shots.

What actually changed this week?

The new promotional material does more than repeat the previously announced sequel. It identifies January 18 as the Japanese television debut and introduces the opening music. That is the news. Season 2 itself was not first announced this week, and the existence of a promotional video does not mean that every episode, the complete English-dub schedule, or a full season-length breakdown has been released.

This distinction matters because a reader arriving from Google may have seen Netflix's October character trailer, the earlier January window, and the latest Japanese promo as though they were interchangeable. They are three separate points in the rollout. The October preview introduced characters and the direction of the story. The latest update answers the narrower question of when Japan's broadcast begins and what song will open it. For the U.S. audience, the next practical confirmation is Netflix's title-page episode listing.

Why the new season's story matters

The setup is more complicated than an exhausted ex-assassin trying to finish an ordinary day at his family store. Netflix's official description says Sakamoto and Shin infiltrate the Japan Clear Creation, or JCC, assassin training institution. They want information about the mysterious figure known as X, also called Slur. Elsewhere, the elite ORDER members Nagumo, Shishiba and Osaragi find themselves drawn into another dangerous assignment.

That brings several threads closer together: the threat against Sakamoto's quiet life, the people who shaped his past, and the larger criminal network that does not disappear simply because he has retired. The series works best when it keeps its unglamorous domestic routine next to action that would be absurd in any normal convenience store. The character comedy gives viewers a reason to care about the fights. Season 2 has an opportunity to give the fights more personal consequences, although trailers are not proof of how particular confrontations will resolve.

Netflix has also introduced Etsuko Satoda, Amane Yotsumura and Satoru Yotsumura in its October character-trailer coverage. These are useful names for returning viewers to recognize; there is no need to turn their appearances into speculative spoilers. If you have finished the anime but not the manga, you can go into the sequel knowing that the JCC and ORDER connections are becoming more important without being told the outcomes of unreleased episodes.

Creepy Nuts is a meaningful choice, not a release-date clue

Creepy Nuts has become a recognizable name to international anime fans, so its involvement is headline-worthy in its own right. But viewers should separate the song reveal from scheduling information. Music news is confirmed when the production names the performer and track; it does not establish when an English dub will appear, how Netflix will package episodes, or whether every market will see the exact same release hour.

The official announcement uses the Japanese song title “Papa wa Imada ni Juvenile.” Translated English renderings may differ between reports, and that is normal. For the site's metadata and social cards, it is safer to use the romanized official name rather than invent an alternate English title. Fans who want to hear the full composition should watch for the artist's own release listing or an official opening-video upload. A fragment in a trailer should not be mislabeled as a complete song release.

Where can U.S. fans watch it?

Season 1, including both parts, is on Netflix. Netflix's official Sakamoto Days materials place Season 2 in January 2027. The newest report gives the Japanese premiere date. The remaining uncertainty is exactly how Netflix will display the upcoming episode schedule in the U.S. and which language tracks will be available at launch. Check Netflix directly near January 18 rather than trusting an automatically generated countdown page.

If you are returning after the first part of Season 1, it is worth finishing the second part before jumping into sequel explainers. It establishes characters and allegiances that the new season will revisit. If you watched everything when it aired, a shorter refresher is enough: remember Sakamoto's promise to protect his family, Shin's connection to him, the danger represented by X, and the difference between ORDER and other assassins. You do not need a forty-item character encyclopedia to enjoy a new trailer.

A useful checklist before the premiere

First, save the Netflix series page rather than a third-party episode calendar. Second, distinguish the Japanese premiere from a confirmed U.S. play time. Third, check for an updated audio-language menu as the date approaches. Fourth, avoid manga-spoiler threads if your plan is to discover the JCC material through the anime. Fifth, keep the November trailer announcement separate from older teasers when discussing what is genuinely new.

Those steps might sound less exciting than promising an unseen fight or predicting an entire season's ending, but they answer what a viewer is likely to need on release day. A good news update makes the next choice easier. For now, January 18 is the newly confirmed date to remember, Creepy Nuts is the newly announced opening act, and Netflix remains the service to watch for U.S. playback details.

Quick answers

Was Season 2 just announced? No. The update is the exact Japanese premiere date and new promotional material. Is the U.S. premiere hour confirmed? Do not assume one from the Japanese broadcast; verify Netflix's U.S. listing. Do I have to read the manga? No; the adaptation is intended to be watched as a series. Will every episode appear at once? Do not assume a binge release without Netflix confirming its format.
