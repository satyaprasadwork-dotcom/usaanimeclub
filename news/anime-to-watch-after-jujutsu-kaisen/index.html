What to Watch After Jujutsu Kaisen: 8 Anime for Fans

Find your next supernatural action series after JJK, from dark fantasy and clever powers to comedy, horror, and emotional character arcs.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 at 2:24 PM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

If you want the closest dark-fantasy blend of curses, dangerous missions, and young fighters, try Chainsaw Man, Hell’s Paradise, or Dorohedoro. For inventive psychic fights with a warmer coming-of-age core, choose Mob Psycho 100. For a more strategic long-form adventure, try Hunter x Hunter. These are editorial recommendations based on tone and structure, not claims that the shows are interchangeable.

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

What anime is most like Jujutsu Kaisen?

For dark supernatural action try Chainsaw Man or Hell’s Paradise; for strange humor try Dorohedoro; for psychic fights with more character growth try Mob Psycho 100.

Start by naming what you want after JJK

Jujutsu Kaisen mixes supernatural rules, close-quarters fights, irreverent humor, and characters who are asked to grow up under pressure. A useful follow-up list should match one or two of those ingredients rather than promise a carbon copy. The shows below cover cursed-world action, grotesque comedy, psychic battles, team combat, and long-form strategy.

The picks are editorial recommendations, not a ranking of quality or a promise that every title is currently on the same US streamer. Episode counts and dubbed tracks vary by season and service. Check the signed-in US listing before starting, especially for series with multiple seasons or new 2026 additions. The table is a shortcut; the notes explain the differences so you can choose by mood.

Pick by what you want more of

For the closest dark-action follow-up, choose Chainsaw Man or Hell's Paradise. Both put young fighters in dangerous worlds where the rules of violence matter, but their tone and setting differ. For the weirdest atmosphere, choose Dorohedoro. For a balance of explosive psychic action and personal growth, Mob Psycho 100 is a strong change of pace.

If you want to stay in battle-shonen territory but shift into a longer quest, Hunter x Hunter offers a much wider range of settings and tactical abilities. Kaiju No. 8 leans toward squad-based monster defense and workplace humor. The right option depends on whether you want to turn the intensity up, add comedy, or take a broader journey after JJK's close focus on curses and sorcerers.

Chainsaw Man: violence, humor, and messy wants

Chainsaw Man is the most direct pick if you want supernatural action that can swing from frightening to absurd in a scene. Denji is not a polished hero with an elaborate mission statement; his immediate wants are basic, and that gives the series a strange emotional anchor amid devils, hunters, and spectacular fights. Crunchyroll's season-one listing is a useful US access check.

Compared with JJK, it is more abrasive and more interested in bodily horror and chaotic desire. It can be a great next show for viewers who liked the way JJK lets comedy and danger coexist, but its graphic content is stronger than many beginner battle series. Check the rating and content notes first. The anime is ongoing as a franchise, so distinguish the available season from later film or sequel material.

Hell’s Paradise: a mission with a brutal fantasy world

Hell's Paradise follows a group of condemned criminals and executioners sent to a mysterious island in search of an elixir. Its first season gives viewers a clear mission structure, a dangerous supernatural environment, and a large ensemble with conflicting motives. That setup suits JJK fans who like a team being forced to solve a high-stakes problem under strange rules.

Its historical-fantasy setting and body-horror imagery make it feel different from a modern school-and-city story. The show has harsh violence and unsettling transformations, so it is not the gentlest recommendation. Start with Season 1 and check the service's listing for the later season status and dub. If the island premise works for you, the mysteries around its ecology and factions become part of the draw.

Dorohedoro: for JJK’s stranger corners

Dorohedoro is a fit if you liked Jujutsu Kaisen's curses and oddball personalities but want a dirtier, more surreal world. A man with a reptile head searches for the sorcerer who transformed him, while two very different communities collide. Its humor is violent and offbeat, and its mystery unfolds through an ensemble rather than one clean school hierarchy.

It is less polished in tone by design: grime, black comedy, food, and magical brutality share the same scene. The 2026 streaming announcement and current catalog should be checked for US access and season coverage. Treat it as a distinctive cult-fantasy option, not an attempt to duplicate JJK's pacing. Viewers who need a straightforward hero arc may prefer Kaiju No. 8 or Demon Slayer.

Mob Psycho 100: explosive fights with a warm center

Mob Psycho 100 is the best pick if you want supernatural combat but also a clear story about emotional maturity. Shigeo Kageyama has extraordinary psychic power, yet his goal is to become a better person and connect with others. Battles are imaginative and visually elastic, while comedy often comes from the gap between power and ordinary social life.

Its emotional direction is warmer and more openly hopeful than JJK's. That makes it an excellent follow-up if you want the supernatural spectacle without another relentless tragedy. The television story is complete, so you can plan a full route rather than wait for a future season. Start at Season 1, watch in order, and save any specials for after the main series.

Hunter x Hunter: strategy and a wider adventure

Hunter x Hunter is a good choice when you want to move from curse battles into a broader adventure built around rules and strategy. Gon begins a journey to become a Hunter, but the story keeps widening its world and changing its kind of challenge. Its power system rewards attention, and confrontations can be as much about choices and constraints as raw strength.

The 2011 adaptation runs much longer than a single season, so this is not the “short break” option. It is a deliberate commitment with several distinct arcs and a satisfying stopping point, although the manga continues beyond the adaptation. Choose it if you are ready for a long story whose tone can shift from bright adventure to darker conflict.

Kaiju No. 8 and Demon Slayer: team action with different flavors

Kaiju No. 8 is a fit if you want a team, a recognizable workplace structure, and battles against giant monsters. Its protagonist is older than the typical school-age lead and joins a defense force after his life changes. The setting has more military science-fiction than occult horror, but the balance of humor, squad dynamics, and action can work for JJK viewers who like ensemble missions.

Demon Slayer offers a clearer heroic quest and a more classical sword-fantasy tone. It is visually spectacular and emotionally direct, with arcs that build toward large confrontations. Its anime has multiple seasons and films, so use a watch-order guide for the Mugen Train film-versus-TV choice and the later arcs. Neither title is a one-season binge, but both provide a more structured route than a loosely connected recommendation list.

A low-friction next-watch path

If you want something closest in intensity, try Chainsaw Man. If you want a completed supernatural series with strong character growth, choose Mob Psycho 100. If you want to stay with cursed-world violence, sample Hell's Paradise or Dorohedoro. If you want a larger adventure and can commit, start Hunter x Hunter. These routes keep the decision small and match likely JJK preferences to clear alternatives.

Before you queue a title, check whether the US service has the whole first season, whether an English dub is included, and what content rating applies. A title page may show one season while a film or sequel sits elsewhere. Most importantly, you do not need your next show to reproduce the exact JJK feeling. A good follow-up can offer a new genre angle while carrying forward one thing you loved.
