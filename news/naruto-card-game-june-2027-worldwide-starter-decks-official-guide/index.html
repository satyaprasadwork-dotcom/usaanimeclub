Naruto Card Game: June 2027 Worldwide Launch, Starter Decks and Official Details

Bandai confirms Naruto Card Game for June 2027 worldwide, with Naruto, Sasuke and Gaara starter decks, first booster, five card types and early-2027 demos.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026 at 3:37 PM UTC

QUICK ANSWER

Bandai's Naruto Card Game is scheduled for a simultaneous worldwide launch in June 2027. Three preconstructed decks are built around Naruto, Sasuke and Gaara; the first booster is titled Enter: Naruto Uzumaki! in Bandai's English press release. The official rules page identifies five card types, a 51-card deck with five Chakra and one Summon card, and a goal of reducing the opposing Leader's Life to zero. Exact retail price and launch day were not provided in the announcement.

Source record: October 10, 2026 · Read the original source

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

When does Bandai's Naruto Card Game release, and which starter decks are confirmed?

Bandai confirms a simultaneous worldwide launch in June 2027 with starter decks for Naruto, Sasuke and Gaara plus an initial booster. The exact launch day and retail prices are not established in the checked original announcement.

June 2027 is the official global launch window

Bandai announced at New York Comic Con in October 2026 that the Naruto Card Game will launch simultaneously worldwide in June 2027. The official English product website repeats the month and the global scope. This is a more specific plan than an undated teaser, but it does not establish a precise calendar day or guarantee that every American retailer will have every item in stock on the same morning.

A global trading-card release can still involve distribution differences by shop, event or region. US fans should distinguish the manufacturer's announced month from a local store's expected shipment or preorder listing. No universal retail price was supplied in the October launch announcement. A marketplace claiming an exact MSRP before Bandai publishes one should be treated cautiously, particularly if it is selling unofficial packs or imported promotional material.

Three starter decks explain how new players begin

The first announced starter products are preconstructed decks based on Naruto Uzumaki, Sasuke Uchiha and Gaara. Bandai describes them as ready to play without building a deck from randomly opened packs. Each has its own leader and associated shinobi, providing an accessible entrance for fans who recognize the characters but have never played a collectible card game. The choice of leaders offers meaningful variation at launch rather than three identical boxes with different covers.

Still, the October details do not specify the final contents of every starter deck in a publicly complete card list. Nor do they establish that a particular starter is competitively superior. A responsible buyer's guide should explain that leader identity is confirmed while the deck's final strategic strength cannot be evaluated before full card lists and tournament rules are available. No pre-release ranking should pretend that an unopened product has already been tested in the retail metagame.

The first booster expands beyond a starter lineup

Bandai's corporate English announcement identifies the first booster as Enter: Naruto Uzumaki!, while the Japanese Naruto franchise's English-language page translates its name as Here Comes Naruto Uzumaki!. These are localized names referring to the initial booster product; an article should make that difference transparent rather than inventing two separate packs. The announced card illustrations feature characters from both Part I and Naruto Shippuden.

Boosters are positioned as a way to acquire additional ninja, techniques and options for building a personal deck. Buying packs is not required merely to try the game if a preconstructed deck is available. The company has not publicly confirmed the final per-pack contents, odds of rare cards or recommended price in the cited October materials. Collectors should wait for official product specifications before treating rumors about chase cards as established facts.

Five card types and how they relate to play

The official rule introduction describes Leader, Character, EX Character, Chakra and Summon cards. Leaders anchor a strategy; Character cards provide the main field presence; some characters can activate Ninjutsu by paying a Chakra cost. EX Characters have their own entry conditions. Chakra cards support techniques, while Summon cards help bring combatants into play. This outlines a tactical system instead of a simple picture-collection product.

The current official site says gameplay uses a 51-card deck, five Chakra cards and one Summon card, and that victory comes from reducing the opponent Leader's Life to zero. These are manufacturer-described rules, not rules invented by watching a short trailer. They provide enough context for US newcomers to recognize the basic objective while leaving room for the complete rulebook to refine precise turn structure, interactions and tournament formats.

Demo events before the retail launch

Bandai plans introductory opportunities during early 2027, including Premier Demo Events at participating trading-card-game stores. A separate wider campaign announced for spring 2027 aims to distribute one million demo decks globally. Those activities are intended to let fans encounter the mechanics before launch and are distinct from the simultaneous June retail release. Some special promotional items are connected to demo events or select Bandai Card Games Fest venues.

Participation details, allocation and local schedules matter. The official Bandai TCG+ app is the logical place to look for participating store events once registration becomes available, but one announcement does not guarantee an event within driving distance of every US reader. A practical editorial update would identify verified participating US locations only after the organizer publishes them, and would not claim every demo deck can be obtained without restrictions.

Artwork and collectibles are part of the appeal

The official product materials show original card art alongside illustrations from the anime and manga. That mix broadens the audience beyond competitive players: some readers may want to collect depictions of their favorite ninjas while others are chiefly interested in deck strategy. Bandai's involvement also differentiates the upcoming game from older Naruto-branded trading cards that may appear in resale listings. The 2027 game has its own confirmed product identity.

Collectors should pay attention to official release art, production notices and authenticity information as those become available. A listing using an unrelated older Naruto card image may describe a different game entirely. Claims about unlimited resale value, guaranteed investment returns or undisclosed rare-card odds would be inappropriate. A trustworthy article can explain what designs have been officially shown without turning the product announcement into investment advice or a manufactured scarcity campaign.

No exact US price or premiere day confirmed in the original reveal

The strongest October facts are the worldwide June 2027 month, the three leader-based starter decks, initial booster, and early demonstration plan. The official website also provides an introduction to rules. Its public-facing announcement does not give a final MSRP for the main launch decks and packs or a universal preorder opening date. Any later retailer figures must be dated and distinguished from Bandai's manufacturer guidance.

Similarly, the phrase worldwide release should not be stretched into a guarantee about every language printed on a first-run card or every store's product lineup. Those are questions worth checking directly on Bandai's regional product pages as launch approaches. For an American buyer, the useful next steps are to monitor the official English website and participating local game shops, not assume unofficial resellers possess authenticated products months early.

How Jump Festa fits into the release roadmap

The Naruto franchise's official Card Game announcement says the next substantial reveal will occur around Jump Festa 2027. The event takes place December 19–20, 2026, ahead of the planned June 2027 global launch. A manufacturer could publish new starter cards, rules or event logistics there, but the original statement does not guarantee a particular unrevealed price or rarity. Editors should publish only what the event actually confirms.

For now, the concise US guide is clear: Bandai's new Naruto Card Game has three ready-to-play starter decks, a first booster, documented core card types and a simultaneous worldwide launch window. An original comparison of the leaders can become useful once real card lists and demos permit it; until then, the practical value lies in verified product identity, rules basics and honest publication of outstanding questions.
