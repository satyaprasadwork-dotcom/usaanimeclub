How Did Itachi Die in Naruto? His Illness, Sasuke's Fight and Final Moments

How did Itachi die, and did Sasuke kill him? A clear explanation of the brothers' final battle, his unnamed illness, Susanoo and the ending's real meaning.

桜
USA Anime Club Editorial

Editor-in-Chief: Satya Prasad

Published November 15, 2026 · Updated November 15, 2026

QUICK ANSWER

Itachi dies at the end of his battle with Sasuke, already weakened by an unnamed illness and enormous strain from fighting and using his Mangekyō abilities. The official Naruto retrospective explicitly describes his illness-ridden body. Sasuke survives and wins the confrontation, but the story does not show him delivering a simple final execution blow. The precise disease is never identified in the main canon.

WHAT TO CHECK NEXT

Did Sasuke defeat Itachi or directly kill him with a finishing blow?

Sasuke survives the duel; Itachi collapses after extreme exertion while seriously ill. The manga does not provide an official diagnosis or a simple finishing strike.

The death scene makes sense only after you know what it hides

Itachi's last approach to Sasuke initially looks like the culmination of a fight over power, eyes and vengeance. Both brothers are exhausted. Itachi reaches toward Sasuke, and the image calls back to a gesture from their childhood. Then he collapses. From Sasuke's position, his life's mission has finally ended. From the audience's later position, almost every assumption behind that victory requires revision. The episode is simultaneously the end of a duel and the beginning of an explanation.

The short answer is not that Sasuke struck a straightforward killing blow. Itachi was seriously ill, had pushed his body through an extraordinarily demanding confrontation and died at its conclusion. Naruto's official retrospective says he continued to fight despite his illness-ridden condition and used his remaining strength for his brother. That is solid evidence for the broad cause. It is not evidence for a named diagnosis, secret medication schedule or exact medical process invented years later by fan theories.

What happened during the Sasuke versus Itachi battle?

The two meet after a long pursuit shaped by Sasuke's desire for revenge. The battle begins with misdirection: apparent attacks and apparently decisive injuries turn out to be genjutsu. That is an appropriate start for Itachi, who has spent much of the series controlling what others believe they have seen. Sasuke responds with planning of his own, using preparation, technique and a growing willingness to force the fight beyond conventional eye-contact exchanges.

The physical stakes then rise sharply. Amaterasu's black flames threaten the battlefield, Sasuke's Kirin uses natural lightning for a devastating attack, and Itachi survives through Susanoo. By the end both have burned through resources that would challenge healthy fighters. Itachi's strength is never only about raw force; he is working toward a hidden goal while allowing Sasuke to experience the battle as the revenge he has earned. A recap that lists only attack names misses this strange second layer of the duel.

Did Sasuke actually kill Itachi?

Sasuke defeated Itachi in the practical sense: he survived the confrontation while Itachi did not, and the fight ended with Itachi falling in front of him. But 'Sasuke killed Itachi' can suggest a final sword strike or a confirmed fatal wound caused by one of Sasuke's attacks. That is not the ending depicted. Itachi approaches his brother, makes a familiar gesture and dies after exhausting what remains of his strength. Later revelations show that Itachi had reasons to shape the outcome.

The difference matters because it lets us avoid two equally misleading extremes. One extreme says Sasuke overpowered a fully healthy Itachi in an ordinary test of strength. The other says Sasuke contributed nothing and Itachi's condition alone determined every exchange. Neither describes what viewers actually see. Sasuke had genuinely grown dangerous; Itachi fought with serious techniques while concealing his priorities; illness and strain shaped the ending. There is no clean tournament result detached from the story's circumstances.

What disease did Itachi have?

The main manga and the official retrospective establish illness without naming a specific disease. Itachi is shown coughing and struggling physically, and his body cannot keep going indefinitely. Popular online explanations assign tuberculosis, lung disease or other specific conditions. Those may be attempts to interpret symptoms, but they are not medically confirmed diagnoses within the core story. A useful guide should state exactly what is known and stop there.

This is particularly important because anime characters do not follow clinical rules. Blood from the mouth, impaired vision, weakness and fatigue may be drawn for dramatic effect rather than to communicate a reliable medical constellation. Some of Itachi's eye strain is associated with repeated Mangekyō Sharingan use, which is distinct from his underlying illness. Nothing in the official source turns those clues into a diagnosis. Presenting one as canon does not make the article more authoritative; it makes it less accurate.

How did his Sharingan abilities affect the fight?

Itachi's Mangekyō techniques are tremendously strong, but they come with costs. Tsukuyomi can distort an opponent's perceived time, Amaterasu produces destructive black flames, and Susanoo creates a formidable spiritual defense and weapon system. Using these powers repeatedly while ill would make the confrontation harder to survive. The official sequence shows Itachi moving from mental warfare into techniques that put growing demands on his deteriorating condition.

That does not mean every moment of weakness can be assigned to a specific jutsu as though the series provided heart-rate data. The more grounded point is cumulative: illness, repeated exertion and an intentionally prolonged confrontation all matter. Susanoo's defense against Sasuke's lightning shows Itachi still had overwhelming capabilities, but he was not an inexhaustible battery. The final gesture carries emotional weight partly because his body is no longer able to sustain the role he has been performing.

What was Itachi trying to do before he died?

One important hidden purpose was to remove Orochimaru's lingering influence from Sasuke. During the battle, that influence emerges, and Itachi uses the legendary sealing ability associated with his Susanoo to eliminate the immediate threat. This recasts the apparently obsessive discussion about Sasuke's eyes. Behind the performance of a villain seeking power is an attempt to remove something dangerous from Sasuke's future. The official Naruto anniversary column emphasizes that freeing Sasuke from the curse mark was part of Itachi's objective.

He also chooses to leave Sasuke with an emotionally legible ending: the hated older brother falls, the younger lives. Unfortunately, this plan depends on Sasuke not learning how the village used Itachi or what happened to the clan. The political truth emerges anyway. What looked like Itachi's final act of control therefore sets off consequences he could not govern. That is the tragic irony of the death scene: even when he gives everything to protect Sasuke, he cannot decide what Sasuke will do with the truth.

The forehead gesture changes the meaning of the scene

Early memories establish a habit of Itachi postponing time with his younger brother and touching Sasuke's forehead affectionately. In the final battle, the gesture appears again. A first-time viewer may read it as a sinister attempt to take Sasuke's eyes, because that is the fear the duel has created. On rewatch, the movement belongs to the brother who once stood inside an ordinary household and shared small moments of tenderness with a child.

This is why the scene stays with viewers longer than many technically larger battles. A gesture is something anyone can understand. You do not need to calculate chakra reserves to see that the frightening figure approaching Sasuke is also someone he spent his early life wanting to impress. Itachi's most consequential communication occurs without a speech explaining the plot. The later explanation does not erase his cruelty toward Sasuke; it makes the final touch a heartbreaking reminder of the relationship both brothers lost.

Did Itachi come back to life later?

He returns temporarily through Kabuto's Reanimation Jutsu during the Fourth Great Ninja War. That is not a cure for his prior illness or a reversal of his original death in the ordinary sense. The revived Itachi is bound by an unnatural technique, and the conflict becomes how to escape the control used to summon him. Shisui's preserved eye and Kotoamatsukami play a crucial role in that escape. Itachi then fights alongside Sasuke against Kabuto.

His second farewell supplies the direct conversation missing from their earlier confrontation. The official Naruto episode I Will Love You Always describes Itachi breaking the reanimation technique and revealing the Uchiha truth before fading. It is a distinct event from his death after the duel. When a question asks 'How many times did Itachi die?' the most responsible answer is that there is one original death and a later reanimated departure. Mixing the two moments creates confusion about chronology.

What the fight reveals about strength, not just tragedy

Fans often use the duel as shorthand for choosing whether Itachi or Sasuke was stronger. But the comparison is structurally unusual. Itachi has advanced eyes, terrifying techniques, a serious illness and hidden intentions. Sasuke has escalating power, preparation and a fierce determination to end what he sees as an unforgivable crime. Their goals are different, even when their attacks collide. One wants to close a chapter under a false narrative; the other wants a confession from an enemy.

That is why a power ranking drawn from the final result is unstable. It does not tell us how an entirely healthy Itachi might fare in a different fight, and the story never stages that alternate version for us to verify. The proper interpretation is about this particular encounter. A character can remain an exceptional fighter while losing because of illness, cost and personal choices. Naruto's writing uses combat to express decisions, not just to determine a hypothetical leaderboard.

Questions about Itachi's death, answered

Was Itachi sick when he fought Sasuke? Yes; the official retrospective explicitly calls his body illness-ridden. Did the manga name his sickness? No specific disease is established there. Did Sasuke land the final blow? The final scene depicts Itachi collapsing after approaching Sasuke, rather than a simple finishing attack. Did Orochimaru play a part? His influence on Sasuke emerges during the duel, and Itachi addresses it as part of his hidden goal.

Was Itachi's death pointless? That is an interpretive question. Itachi succeeds in ending Orochimaru's immediate grip and leaving Sasuke alive, but fails to prevent Sasuke from learning the massacre's political truth and pursuing a new form of vengeance. Is the reanimated farewell the same death? No; it occurs much later during the war. For the original confrontation, the Naruto official archive cites manga chapters in volumes 42–44, while the later farewell is documented separately.
